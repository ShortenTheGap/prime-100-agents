# Stage 1 Reference: The Ideal Buyer Persona

## What This Stage Produces

An 11-section Ideal Buyer Persona saved as `01_ideal-buyer-persona.md`.
The Persona is the foundation everything else sits on. Without it,
every future content session produces generic output. With it, every
session produces output calibrated to one specific buyer.

## The 8 Intake Questions

Ask these in order, one at a time. Wait for each answer.

1. What does your business do? (1-2 sentences)
2. What's your average revenue per client?
3. What's the typical revenue stage of your buyer? ($50K/mo, $100K/mo,
   $250K/mo, $1M/mo?)
4. What's the team size of your buyer?
5. What's the ONE biggest problem your work solves for them?
6. Name 3 real clients (first names ok) and one specific result for
   each
7. What do your buyers say when they first contact you? (Their actual
   words)
8. What does your buyer believe that's wrong, that keeps them stuck?

If the operator can't answer any question with specifics, push back.
Don't accept "everyone" or "small businesses" as a target. Force
specificity. If they can't name 3 real clients with real results, stop
and flag for escalation per the SKILL.md rules.

## The 11 Sections

Build the Persona in this exact structure.

### Section 1: Demographics
- Revenue range
- Team size range
- Industry or vertical
- Geographic concentration (if any)
- Years in business (typical)

### Section 2: The Core Problem
The ONE specific problem the operator's work solves. Not 3 problems.
One. Stated in the buyer's own words if possible.

### Section 3: Top 3 Fears
What keeps the buyer up at night about this problem. Specific,
emotional, written in their voice.

### Section 4: Relationship Impacts
How this problem affects their team, their family, their reputation,
their identity. The hidden costs.

### Section 5: Motivation Triggers
What makes them finally take action. The specific moment, event, or
threshold that converts "thinking about it" into "doing something."

### Section 6: Desired Transformations
The before-and-after picture. What changes in their business, life,
identity when the problem is solved.

### Section 7: Failed Solutions
What they've already tried that didn't work. Tools, agencies, hires,
courses. Why each one failed them.

### Section 8: Top 5 Objections
The exact reasons they'll say no, in their own language. Including the
ones they won't say out loud.

### Section 9: Daily Reality
A typical day in their business. Who they talk to. What stresses them.
What energizes them. Where they spend their time.

### Section 10: Market Specifics
Industry-specific dynamics, competitors, market conditions affecting
them right now.

### Section 11: The One-Sentence Compression
Compress the whole Persona into one sentence using this template:

"My buyer is a [specific role/industry/revenue level] who is currently
[specific demand-side problem] and wants [specific outcome] but
believes [false belief that keeps them stuck]."

This sentence is non-negotiable. Every Persona ends with it. Copy it
into the index note once the Persona is locked.

## Stage 1 Rules

1. Force specificity. No "small business owners" or "entrepreneurs."
   Get to a specific role at a specific revenue level.
2. Use the operator's own words wherever possible. Don't paraphrase
   what they said.
3. Never invent details the operator didn't provide. If a section is
   thin, mark it as "[Needs more input from operator]."
4. Save with the frontmatter and relative-markdown-link format defined in SKILL.md.
