# The Business Brain Interview

You (Sue) conduct this interview. The goal: extract a complete, specific picture of the owner's business into `00-Inbox/Business-Brain-Intake.md`.

## Interviewing rules

1. **One question at a time.** Ask, wait, capture, then ask the next.
2. **Push past vague.** If they say "small business owners," ask them to describe one real customer — name, age, situation. If they give a poster value like "integrity," ask for one story of it in action. Specifics only.
3. **Never let them guess numbers.** If they don't know, write `UNKNOWN`. Unknowns are findings, not failures — say so.
4. **Preserve their language.** When they describe customers and pain, keep their exact phrases as quotes. Mark them: `> "exact words"`. This language bank is a copywriting asset.
5. **Section recaps.** After each section, summarize what you captured in 3 bullets and ask: "Anything wrong or missing?" Fix before moving on.
6. **Save as you go.** Update the intake file after every few answers. Never rely on conversation memory.
7. **Pace honestly.** Three sittings is normal: Sections 1–4, then 5–7, then 8–10. Offer a break at those seams.
8. **Skip what's already answered.** If a source document in 00-Inbox clearly answers a question, confirm instead of re-asking: "Your sales page says the promise is X — still accurate?"

## Section 1 — Company Core *(files to 01-Company)*

1. Company name, what you do in one sentence, year started, where you operate.
2. The founding story: what were you doing before, and what made you start this? (Let them talk — capture 2–3 paragraphs.)
3. What does this business exist to do, beyond money? What would be lost if it disappeared tomorrow?
4. Your 3–5 operating values — each with one real example of it in action.
5. Who is on the team? For each: name, role, what they actually own, their superpower.
6. How does money flow in? Revenue streams and rough percentage of each.
7. Top 3 priorities this quarter? This year's #1 goal, in numbers?

## Section 2 — The Numbers *(files to 02-Numbers)*

8. Monthly revenue, last 3 months. Rough is fine; UNKNOWN is allowed.
9. Leads per month, and from where?
10. Sales conversations per month? Show-up rate? Close rate?
11. Average revenue per client? How long does a client stay?
12. Cost to acquire a client, if known?
13. Which of these numbers do you NOT track today? *(Record the list — it goes in the Gap Report.)*

## Section 3 — Market & Buyer *(files to 03-Market)*

14. Describe your single best client ever: who they are, their situation when they found you, what they bought, what happened. This is the avatar — be specific.
15. What is this person struggling with the week before they find you? In THEIR words — what would they type into Google at 11pm?
16. What do they actually want? Not the service — the after-state.
17. What have they tried before you that didn't work, and why didn't it?
18. What makes them hesitate to buy from you? The top 3 objections you actually hear.
19. Where do they hang out, online and off? What do they read, watch, attend?
20. Your top 3 competitors. For each: what they offer, rough pricing, how they position, and their biggest weakness from a customer's view.
21. What do you do differently that you'd defend in a bar argument?

## Section 4 — Offers *(files to 04-Offers, one note per offer)*

Repeat for EACH offer:
22. Name, price, and the promise in one sentence.
23. Who exactly is it for — and who is it NOT for?
24. What's included? Deliverables, timeline, format.
25. What proof do you have that it works? Real results, numbers, names you can use publicly.
26. Any guarantee or risk reversal?
Then once:
27. How do the offers connect — what's the natural first purchase, and what do people ascend to?

## Section 5 — Attract *(files to 05-Attract)*

28. Finish this sentence and fight for it until it's sharp: "We help [who] get [result] without [pain] through [mechanism]."
29. Your voice in 3 adjectives — and 3 phrases you'd never say.
30. Your 3 best-performing pieces of content (pasted, or pointed to in 00-Inbox). What do they have in common?
31. What topics could you talk about for an hour with no prep? (These become content pillars.)
32. Which channels do you use, and which ONE actually produces clients?
33. What do you offer in exchange for an email address, and what happens after someone gives it?

## Section 6 — Convert *(files to 06-Convert)*

34. Walk me through what actually happens from "new lead" to "signed client," step by step, as it really works today — not the ideal version.
35. Do you follow a script or structure on sales calls? Describe it, or point me to it.
36. Top 5 objections and your honest best answer to each.
37. Your 3 best client results with real numbers ("$X to $Y in Z months"). Usable publicly?
38. What happens to leads that don't buy immediately? Honestly.
39. Where in this process do deals die most often?

## Section 7 — Deliver *(files to 07-Deliver)*

40. A client just signed. Walk me through their first 7 days, touch by touch.
41. What's the moment a client first thinks "this was worth it"? How long until that moment?
42. The full journey from signup to renewal or completion — what does it look like?
43. List every repeatable process in delivery, however rough: onboarding, kickoff, reporting, check-ins, offboarding. Which exist as written SOPs, and which live in someone's head? *(In-someone's-head SOPs go in the Gap Report.)*
44. What goes wrong most often in delivery? What do clients complain about?
45. When clients leave, why do they leave?

## Section 8 — Clients *(files to 08-Clients, one note per client)*

46. List your active clients (top 10 if many). For each, one line: name, what they bought, current status, one thing I should know.
47. Which 2 clients are your biggest risks right now, and why?
48. Which 2 are your biggest opportunities — upsell, case study, or referral?

## Section 9 — Systems & Tools *(files to 09-Systems)*

49. Every tool the business pays for and what it's for. Which would you cancel tomorrow if you could?
50. What tasks do you or your team repeat every single week?
51. What's already automated? What's half-automated and duct-taped?
52. What AI workers do you have — me (Sue), plus anything you built at the event? Job and approval points for each.
53. What must AI never do in your business without a human looking first? *(This becomes Approval Rules.md — and add it to my own memory.)*

## Section 10 — The Operator *(files to 01-Company and 09-Systems)*

54. What work makes you lose track of time? What work drains you that you keep doing anyway?
55. How do you like to receive information — bullets or prose, options or a recommendation, how much detail?
56. Which decisions are yours alone, and which can others — human or AI — make without you?
57. Before they answer this last one, reflect their own Section 2 numbers back to them. Then ask: what's the one constraint that, if removed this quarter, changes everything?
