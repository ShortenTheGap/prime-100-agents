# Peter Drucker: Skills Roadmap (Phase Two)

> Index of the discrete skills that give Peter his hands. Each becomes `skills/<skill-name>/SKILL.md`, loaded on top of the core skill. Status: **proposed** until built. Agree the list, then build in the order below.

Each entry: what it produces, when it triggers, the Drucker principles it rests on (dossier section), and the modern layer we add.

---

## Existing Prime skills Peter owns or uses (not new builds)

These already exist. Peter uses them as they are; the new skills below call them rather than duplicate them.

- **`prime-time-audit`** (Prime CEO Time Audit). Guided interview that classifies every task by Tier (Admin / Technician / Manager / Exec), Energy, and CDS (Cut / Delegate / Systemize), then totals hours and reclaimable time. Peter treats it as Drucker's time log (dossier §4.7, step 1) and adds the diagnosis on top. Owner: Peter.
- **The Replacement Matrix** (Prime operating system framework). Sorts tasks by frequency and judgement to decide automation versus agent versus person. Peter uses it after the time audit and inside `sop-builder`; the build goes to Manny.

## Tier 1: the foundation (build first; everything else depends on them)

### 1. `five-questions`
- **Produces:** the member's answers to the Five Most Important Questions (mission, customer, what the customer values, results, plan), a one-paragraph business definition, and the "What will it be? What should it be?" note.
- **Triggers:** "what should I focus on," "I don't know what my business is anymore," "mission," "who is my customer," "strategy," "annual plan," "we're doing too many things"; also auto-runs when any planning request arrives and memory has no confirmed answers.
- **Drucker:** §4.1, §4.2, §4.3.
- **Modern layer:** pulls customer value from the Ideal Buyer Profile and Living Avatar; shares the result with David so marketing and operations rest on the same definition.

### 2. `time-diagnosis`
- **Produces:** the Drucker layer on top of a `prime-time-audit` result: answers to the three diagnostic questions, recurrent crises named, discretionary time found and consolidated into protected blocks, and a first stop list.
- **Triggers:** "where does my time go," "I'm drowning," "I work too many hours," "what should I get off my plate"; runs after `prime-time-audit` completes.
- **Drucker:** §4.7 in full; §4.6 practice 1.
- **Modern layer:** calendar block rules handed to Sue; CDS Systemize items routed to `sop-builder`; Replacement Matrix applied to Delegate items.

### 3. `quarterly-objectives`
- **Produces:** quarterly objectives under management by objectives and self-control: proposed objectives per owner across the relevant key areas, measures outside the business, posteriorities, and a manager's-letter template for each owner to rewrite.
- **Triggers:** "quarterly goals," "OKRs," "objectives," "targets," "what are we working on this quarter," "goal setting."
- **Drucker:** §4.4 (eight key areas), §4.5 (MBO and self-control, the manager's letter), §4.6 practice 4.
- **Modern layer:** OKR-compatible format; one or two priorities per quarter for small firms; review dates on the calendar via Sue.

### 4. `abandonment-review`
- **Produces:** a stop list: every offer, client type, channel, meeting, tool, and commitment in scope put on trial with the abandonment question, the verdict, and the exit step for each.
- **Triggers:** "what should I stop doing," "we're stretched thin," "should we kill this offer," "too many projects," "simplify"; also runs inside every annual plan.
- **Drucker:** §4.10 (planned abandonment), §4.11 (the business X-ray, investments in managerial ego).
- **Modern layer:** tool and subscription sprawl; AI tool audit; hands revenue impact of abandoning an offer to Charlie.

### 5. `decision-memo`
- **Produces:** a one-page decision memo: generic or exceptional, problem definition, boundary conditions, what is right, the dissenting view, the action built in, and the feedback date.
- **Triggers:** "should I," "help me decide," "I can't make up my mind," "pros and cons," "big decision."
- **Drucker:** §4.8 in full; Sloan's disagreement case (§5).
- **Modern layer:** a decision journal entry for feedback analysis; generic decisions converted into a standing rule; financial decisions handed to Charlie with the operating frame.

## Tier 2: the operating machinery

### 6. `role-definition`
- **Produces:** role charters: the result the role is for, the key contributions, decisions the role owns, measures, the strengths it requires, and what the role is not.
- **Triggers:** "job description," "role," "who does what," "responsibilities," "I need to define this position."
- **Drucker:** §4.6 practice 2 (contribution), §4.9 (think through the assignment; widow-maker jobs).
- **Modern layer:** roles for AI agents as well as people; contractor versus employee flag for professional review.

### 7. `delegation-brief`
- **Produces:** a delegation brief in the form of the manager's letter: the result, the standard, the deadline, the authority given, the known obstacles, what the delegate needs from the member, and the check-in date.
- **Triggers:** "delegate," "hand this off," "get this off my plate," "brief my VA," "brief my team."
- **Drucker:** §4.5 (the manager's letter), §4.7 (who else could do it as well or better).
- **Modern layer:** briefs written so that a person or an AI agent can execute from them; hands calendar check-ins to Sue.

### 8. `sop-builder`
- **Produces:** standard operating procedures for generic, recurring work: purpose and customer, trigger, steps, owner, quality standard, exceptions and who decides them, and the measure; plus the Replacement Matrix verdict and, where it says automate, a process spec for Manny.
- **Triggers:** "SOP," "process," "checklist," "how do we do X every time," "this keeps going wrong," "systemize."
- **Drucker:** §4.8 (generic problems need rules), §4.7 (the recurrent crisis), §3 (effectiveness before efficiency).
- **Modern layer:** Replacement Matrix applied step by step; automation specs handed to Manny; SOPs stored in the Business Brain.

### 9. `operating-rhythm`
- **Produces:** the meeting and reporting cadence: each meeting with one purpose, one owner, attendees, inputs, and a written follow-up; the weekly, monthly, and quarterly review loop; what reports go to whom for self-control.
- **Triggers:** "meetings," "too many meetings," "team cadence," "weekly meeting," "standup," "reporting," "how should we run our week."
- **Drucker:** §4.14 in full; §4.5 (information goes to the person doing the work); §2 (the editor's six-monthly review).
- **Modern layer:** async updates in place of status meetings; meeting notes via `prime-meeting-notes` where installed; Sue applies the cadence to the calendar.

### 10. `results-dashboard`
- **Produces:** a short set of KPIs that look outside the business (customer outcomes, retention, referrals, delivery quality) alongside the few inside measures that matter, each with an owner and a review rhythm; plus the "what we will not measure" list.
- **Triggers:** "KPIs," "metrics," "scorecard," "dashboard," "how do I know if it's working," "what should I track."
- **Drucker:** §4.11 (results exist only outside), §4.5 (measurement for self-control), §10 (the real nuance behind "what gets measured").
- **Modern layer:** financial KPIs specified with Charlie; build of any dashboard handed to Manny.

## Tier 3: people, structure, and the future

### 11. `people-decision`
- **Produces:** a hiring or placement scorecard following the five steps: the assignment thought through, three to five candidates, strengths evidence, references from people who worked with each, and the ninety-day "what does the job require" check.
- **Triggers:** "hire," "who should I hire," "next hire," "promote," "this person isn't working out," "should I let someone go."
- **Drucker:** §4.9 in full.
- **Modern layer:** person versus agent versus automation as the first question (via the time audit); all firing, discipline, and classification questions flagged for professional advice; compensation numbers to Charlie.

### 12. `strengths-review`
- **Produces:** a founder or team-member strengths review using Managing Oneself: strengths from feedback analysis, how the person performs, values, where they belong, what they should contribute; and a decision-journal template for the next nine to twelve months.
- **Triggers:** "what am I good at," "what should I stop doing myself," "my role as CEO," "I'm the bottleneck," "zone of genius."
- **Drucker:** §4.13 (Managing Oneself), §4.6 practice 3, §2 (the seven experiences).
- **Modern layer:** pairs with the Tier 4 Exec column of the time audit; Prime's Zone of Genius framework referenced where installed.

### 13. `org-review`
- **Produces:** a review of how the work is structured: key activities, who owns each result, decisions and where they are made, relations, and what the structure is compensating for; three ranked changes.
- **Triggers:** "org chart," "structure," "my team is a mess," "nobody owns anything," "we've grown and it's chaos."
- **Drucker:** §4.9, §4.14, `[Corporation 1946]` and `[Tasks 1973]` on structure (dossier §1, §5).
- **Modern layer:** scaled to firms of one to fifty; AI agents shown on the chart as workers with owners.

### 14. `theory-of-the-business`
- **Produces:** an assumptions audit: environment, mission, and core competencies written down; tested against reality, against each other, against non-customers, and against recent unexpected results; a list of assumptions to retest.
- **Triggers:** "what worked before isn't working," "the market changed," "annual review," "strategy reset," "why are we stuck."
- **Drucker:** §4.10 (theory of the business, preventive care, early diagnosis).
- **Modern layer:** AI's effect on the member's market treated as an environment assumption; customer-language evidence from the Living Avatar.

### 15. `innovation-scan`
- **Produces:** a quarterly scan of the seven sources of innovative opportunity for the member's market, with the unexpected successes and failures first, and one to three opportunities ranked, each with a small first test.
- **Triggers:** "new offer ideas," "where's the opportunity," "how do we grow," "innovation," "what should we launch next."
- **Drucker:** §4.12 in full; §4.11 (feed opportunities).
- **Modern layer:** web research briefed to `research-agent`; offer design handed to David; economics to Charlie.

## Build order and rationale

1 to 5 first: every other skill rests on the business definition, the time diagnosis, objectives, abandonment, and the decision process. 6 to 10 next: these are what members most often ask for, and they turn decisions into owned, repeatable work. 11 to 15 last: they shape the people and the future of the business once the operating basics hold.

## Conventions for every Peter skill

- Frontmatter `name` and a pushy `description` that lists the trigger phrases.
- Body under 500 lines; deeper material in `references/`.
- Every rule tagged with its dossier section so it can be checked and challenged.
- A hand-back template ending in **Stop doing / Why this / What I am unsure of / First step / Look at results on / Facts I need / Handoffs**.
- Explicit "modern layer" notes marked as extrapolation from Drucker's large-corporation, pre-software findings.
- Calls existing Prime skills (`prime-time-audit`, the Replacement Matrix) rather than rebuilding them.
- Never builds automations (Manny), makes financial calls (Charlie), writes marketing (David), or acts on inbox and calendar (Sue).
- Never sends, publishes, schedules, or fabricates. No em dashes or en dashes.
