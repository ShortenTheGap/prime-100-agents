# David Ogilvy — Skills Roadmap (Phase Two)

> Index of the discrete skills that give David his hands. Each becomes `skills/<skill-name>/SKILL.md`, loaded on top of the core skill. Status: **proposed** until built. Agree the list, then build in the order below.

Each entry: what it produces, when it triggers, the Ogilvy principles it rests on (dossier section), and the modern layer we add.

---

## Tier 1 — the foundation (build first; everything else depends on them)

### 1. `positioning-statement`
- **Produces:** the positioning block (who / what / category / one thing / reason to believe), a competitor-substitution test, and 3 alternative positions with trade-offs.
- **Triggers:** "position," "positioning," "who is this for," "how do I differentiate," "what makes us different," "we're just like X but…"; also auto-runs when any copy request arrives with no position.
- **Ogilvy:** §4.3 (Dove, SAAB), §4.10 (homework before positioning), Commandment 1.
- **Modern layer:** ICP definition, category design vs. category entry, jobs-to-be-done framing, pricing tier implications.

### 2. `research-brief` (the homework skill)
- **Produces:** a fact sheet (every claim + source), a competitor advertising teardown (promises made, positions taken, gaps), and a customer-language summary; a list of what is missing and how to get it.
- **Triggers:** start of any campaign or flagship asset; "research," "what are competitors saying," "before we write."
- **Ogilvy:** §4.10, §4.11 (Calt letter), Rolls-Royce three weeks.
- **Modern layer:** review mining, transcript mining (pairs with `living-avatar`), ad-library teardowns, SERP intent; delegates web work to `research-agent`.

### 3. `brand-image-definition`
- **Produces:** a brand personality statement (3–5 adjectives with do/don't examples), a consistency charter (what never changes across assets), a 5-year image ambition, and a one-page brand voice draft that can populate `shared/brand-voice.md`.
- **Triggers:** "brand," "brand voice," "tone," "how should we sound," "we keep changing our look," new business, rebrand.
- **Ogilvy:** §4.4 (1955 speech, Commandment 10), §4.2 (thirty-year test).
- **Modern layer:** visual identity direction for designers/AI image tools, founder-led brand, cross-channel consistency rules.

### 4. `headline-writing`
- **Produces:** 20+ headlines classified by mechanism, a shortlist of 3–5 with reasoning, subhead pairings, and the recommended A/B test.
- **Triggers:** "headline," "hook," "subject line," "title," "first line," "H1," "opening," "thumbnail text."
- **Ogilvy:** §4.5 in full; §4.11 (26 headlines).
- **Modern layer:** channel variants — email subject lines, ad primary text, landing H1, video first three seconds, post openers; character limits; preview-text pairing.

### 5. `ad-critique`
- **Produces:** a structured review (intent → headline → positioning → copy → visual → ask), a score against Ogilvy's checks, three ranked changes, and an offer to rewrite.
- **Triggers:** member pastes or uploads any existing marketing asset; "review," "critique," "what do you think," "why isn't this converting," "roast this."
- **Ogilvy:** CLAUDE.md critique mode; §4.5–4.8; §8 voice.
- **Modern layer:** channel-specific checks (mobile fold, mute test, scroll depth), conversion diagnostics, compliance flags.

## Tier 2 — the money assets

### 6. `long-form-sales-copy`
- **Produces:** long-form sales pages, advertorials, sales letters — full structure (headline, lead, story/problem, promise, proof, offer, guarantee, ask, P.S.), with proof placeholders flagged.
- **Triggers:** "sales page," "landing page for [high-ticket]," "advertorial," "long-form," "sales letter," "webinar registration page."
- **Ogilvy:** §4.6 (long copy sells; complete sale; testimonials; advice), §4.9 (the letter; offer; guarantee), Rolls-Royce 719 words, Merrill Lynch 6,450 words.
- **Modern layer:** mobile-first section order, scannability, proof stacking, objection sequencing, CTA repetition, page-speed-aware media notes.

### 7. `direct-response-offer`
- **Produces:** offer architecture — the core promise, price framing, bonuses that remove specific objections, guarantee wording, real deadline/limit mechanics, and the response device.
- **Triggers:** "offer," "what should I charge," "bonus," "guarantee," "how do I make this irresistible," "launch."
- **Ogilvy:** §4.9 (offer and guarantee lift response more than writing tricks; test), Commandment 9 (honesty).
- **Modern layer:** value-stacking without hype, risk-reversal variants, ethical urgency rules, payment-plan framing.

### 8. `email-copy`
- **Produces:** single emails and sequences (welcome, nurture, launch, re-engagement, post-purchase) with subject/preview pairs and one-ask structure.
- **Triggers:** "email," "sequence," "newsletter," "follow-up," "nurture," "drip."
- **Ogilvy:** §4.9 (the letter as the most powerful DR component; long, personal, one-to-one), §4.12 (write the way you talk; crystal clear ask).
- **Modern layer:** deliverability-safe formatting, plain-text vs. designed, segmentation hooks, cadence, GHL/n8n handoff notes.

### 9. `paid-ad-copy`
- **Produces:** static and video ad copy for Meta/Google/LinkedIn/YouTube — primary text, headline, description, CTA, with 3–5 angle variants per audience and visual direction.
- **Triggers:** "ad," "Facebook ad," "Meta ad," "Google ad," "LinkedIn ad," "ad copy," "creative for paid."
- **Ogilvy:** §4.5 (headline rules), §4.7 (posters: one picture, few words; story appeal), §4.8 (vampire video; show product early).
- **Modern layer:** hook-first structure, angle/audience matrices, ad-library differentiation, platform limits, creative-testing plan.

### 10. `video-script`
- **Produces:** scripts for VSLs, ads, demos, and short-form video — with supers, on-screen text, and shot notes; a mute-test pass.
- **Triggers:** "script," "VSL," "video," "reel," "short," "YouTube ad," "demo video," "explainer."
- **Ogilvy:** §4.8 in full (demonstration, problem–solution, testimonial, talking head, continuing character; picture carries the sale; supers match VO; no vampire video).
- **Modern layer:** first-three-seconds hook, captions as the copy, retention structure, creator/UGC formats, length by placement.

## Tier 3 — proof, brand assets, and craft

### 11. `testimonial-and-proof`
- **Produces:** a proof inventory, testimonial edits that stay verbatim (light redaction only), case-study structure, proof placement plan; identifies proof gaps and the questions to gather it.
- **Triggers:** "testimonials," "case study," "social proof," "reviews," "results," "credibility."
- **Ogilvy:** §4.6 (testimonials in the customer's idiom beat copywriter prose; celebrity caution), Commandment 3 (facts).
- **Modern layer:** screenshot proof, UGC, specificity standards, legal/earnings-claim flags; integrates with `living-avatar` Quote Bank.

### 12. `tagline-and-naming`
- **Produces:** taglines, product/offer names, and signature lines — 20+ options, screened by the thirty-year test and the "mere slogan" trap.
- **Triggers:** "tagline," "slogan," "name for," "what should we call," "signature line."
- **Ogilvy:** §3 (a mere slogan persuades nobody — a tagline must carry a promise), §4.2 (thirty-year test), §4.4.
- **Modern layer:** domain/handle availability notes (via research), trademark-risk flags, pronounceability, category clarity.

### 13. `visual-direction`
- **Produces:** art-direction briefs for designers or AI image/video tools — hero concept with story appeal, layout order, typography guidance, do/don't list.
- **Triggers:** "what should the image be," "design brief," "layout," "creative direction," "thumbnail," "hero image."
- **Ogilvy:** §4.7 in full (photographs; story appeal; product in use; picture → headline → copy; editorial look; typography).
- **Modern layer:** mobile fold, thumb-stop, image-prompt phrasing for generative tools, accessibility (contrast, alt text).

### 14. `campaign-brief`
- **Produces:** a one-to-two-page creative brief (objective, audience, positioning, promise, proof, mandatories, tone, deliverables, success metric, test plan) that any writer or agent can execute from.
- **Triggers:** "campaign," "launch plan," "brief," "we're planning to…," anything spanning multiple assets.
- **Ogilvy:** §4.11 (definition of the problem and statement of purpose before creative), §4.1, §4.12 (never more than two pages).
- **Modern layer:** channel plan, asset list mapped to funnel stage, handoffs to `content-agent`/`ghl-agent`.

### 15. `ogilvy-edit`
- **Produces:** a line edit of any prose — copy, emails, briefs, scripts — under the 1982 memo: shorter words, shorter sentences, jargon removed, ask made explicit; tracked-changes style with reasons.
- **Triggers:** "edit this," "tighten," "make this clearer," "too long," "sounds corporate."
- **Ogilvy:** §4.12 (all ten points), §4.6 (no superlatives; specifics).
- **Modern layer:** readability targets, reading-level notes, plain-language compliance.

---

## Build order and rationale

1–5 first: every other skill calls positioning, homework, brand, headlines, or critique. 6–10 next: these are what members most often ask for and where the revenue is. 11–15 last: they sharpen the rest.

## Conventions for every David skill

- Frontmatter `name` and a pushy `description` that lists the trigger phrases.
- Body under 500 lines; deeper material in `references/`.
- Every craft rule tagged with its dossier section so it can be checked and challenged.
- A hand-back template ending in **Why this one / What I am unsure of / Test first / Facts I need**.
- Explicit "modern layer" notes marked as extrapolation from Ogilvy's print/TV findings.
- Never sends, publishes, or fabricates.
