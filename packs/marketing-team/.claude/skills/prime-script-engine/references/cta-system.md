# CTA System: Decision Tree, Types, and Alternatives

Definition (Devin's, verbatim concept): a clear request or instruction encouraging viewers to take the next step, on-platform (engagement) or off-platform. The CTA is where earned attention gets DIRECTED. Not every video gets one.

Known gap: the "CTA Execution Guidelines" module (when to use, how to build the perfect one, common mistakes) is not in the corpus. The rules below come from the CTA Intro, Types of CTAs, and CTA Alternatives modules. Where execution judgment is needed beyond these rules, say so rather than inventing gospel.

## The decision tree

```
1. Does this video need a CTA?
   NO  → use an alternative: Loop or Snap (see below)
   YES → continue
2. WHERE does it sit?
   Default: end-roll (higher conversion: value fully built, natural next step)
   Mid-roll ONLY when one of three conditions holds:
     a. The video has a loop (the end slot is taken, move the CTA inside)
     b. Pattern interrupt (long monotone list: break it mid-way with the CTA,
        e.g. after hook 4 of 7: "Future Devin here. I made a list of 30 viral
        hooks, comment [KEYWORD] and I'll send it")
     c. Step extension (a tutorial step has an automatable upgrade too long to
        teach: "Want the prompt that does this automatically? Comment [KEYWORD]")
   Mid-roll trade: seen by MORE people (watch-time decay), converts WORSE.
3. WHAT does it ask?
   Instagram → engagement CTA ~99% of the time, almost always
     "comment [KEYWORD]" → ManyChat auto-DMs the link. Comments feed the
     algorithm AND capture the person in DMs. Bio-link asks count as engagement.
   TikTok / YouTube → off-platform allowed, including cross-platform pulls
     ("DM me [KEYWORD] on Instagram") that feed the IG DM funnel.
4. Conversion levers: quality of the value, specificity of the ask,
   how much they like you.
```

Script shapes: end-roll = Hook → Value → CTA. Mid-roll = Hook → Value → CTA → rest of Value.

## the entrepreneur-specific routing
Podcast/show CTAs route through DMjoe.com with keywords AGENCY, ARCHITECT, or PRIME by show type. Short-form keyword CTAs follow the ManyChat pattern above. The $97 Content Engine Workshop is the standing gateway offer for cold audiences.

## CTA alternatives (when the video is better with no ask)

Both alternatives force a replay. Replays are a tracked metric, and every re-watched second stacks watch time. Never force a weak CTA; loop or snap instead.

### The Loop
The ending flows seamlessly back into the hook.
Construction (mechanical, follow exactly):
1. Write ONE complete sentence.
2. Split it: the SECOND half is the hook, the FIRST half is the ending.
3. Record the full sentence in one take with a micro-pause at the split.
4. Editor cuts at the pause: second half to the front, first half to the end.
5. On replay, the ending flows grammatically into the hook.
Example: "Do this 20 times over, and that's | how to make 20 viral videos in 20 minutes, explained in 20 seconds." (Hook = right of the bar, ending = left.)
Loop scripts get a mid-roll CTA if they need a CTA at all (condition 2a).

### The Snap
End abruptly and unpredictably, mid-thought, right after the value lands. The viewer can't sense the ending coming, can't scroll away early, and the replay starts before they react. The MrBeast ending.

## Output requirement
Every optimized script ends with an explicit ending decision, labeled: END-ROLL CTA / MID-ROLL CTA (with which condition justified it) / LOOP (with the split sentence written out) / SNAP (with the exact cut point marked).


## Promise-CTA adjacency (field gospel, from tested coaching feedback)
Never place a Promise component directly before a CTA. Their testing: promise-then-CTA converts WORSE than CTA alone; by CTA time the value's self-evidence has done the selling, and a promise line reads as one more pitch between the viewer and the thing they already want. Rules:
- Video ends with a CTA → no promise beat immediately before it. Cut it or move it earlier in the script if it earns its seconds there.
- Video ends WITHOUT a CTA → a short promise works as the bow.
