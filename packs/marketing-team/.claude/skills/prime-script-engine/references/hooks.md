# Hooks: Verbal + Visual

**Before writing any hook: fetch the live hook bank.** Search the entrepreneur's Google Drive for the doc named "Prime Hook Bank" (or ask the entrepreneur for the latest if Drive is unavailable). It holds current viral hook templates and interest peak patterns, updated monthly from new template drops and continuously from Sandcastles analyses. Treat every entry as RAW MATERIAL under Sin 11: never use a template verbatim; identify why it hits (the "mechanism" field), then apply one of the three moves (presentation / wording / angle) to make it the entrepreneur's. If the bank can't be retrieved, proceed on the principles below and say so.

Two layers to every hook: the verbal hook (the spoken line, always first in the script) and the visual hook (its on-screen representation). Every script ships with both fields filled in; the creator writes both, never the editor.

Sourcing note: BOTH layers are now gospel. The verbal hook system comes from the Scientific Hook Masterclass V2 (the R × A × C × U^B formula below); the visual hook system from the dedicated three-module series.

## Part 1: The verbal hook

Job: grab attention in the first seconds. The single most important line in the script. But per Interest Peak Mastery: the hook only gets people to CONSIDER watching; if the hook can't carry full buy-in alone, a double-down interest peak backs it up (script-structure.md).

**The formula: R × A × C × U^B.** The foundation terms multiply, so a zero anywhere kills the hook. U and B are the multipliers that separate 10K from 1M.

- **R: Relevant.** Value only exists relative to a person. Anchor to the mascot doc, and target the problems the mascot THINKS he has, not the ones you know he has (hook into the problem the mascot FEELS, phrased in the mascot doc's own-words section). Pick the content idea (the narrow problem) BEFORE hooking anything. **Operational test (mandatory in the gauntlet): the internal monologue test. Would the mascot ever say this desire/problem TO HIMSELF in these words?** "I wish I had more sales" passes; "I wish I had words that make strangers buy" fails (clever synthetic phrase, not a felt desire). Match his vocabulary level too: don't upgrade to sophisticated phrasing ("higher-converting copy") the mascot doesn't use. An R failure disqualifies a candidate regardless of its U score; uniqueness never outranks a real felt desire.
- **A: Awareness (the biggest failure point).** Four levels: symptom aware (feels effects, doesn't know the cause) → problem aware (knows the problem, not the fix) → solution aware (knows a fix exists, not how to apply it) → product aware (knows fixes, can't choose). Run the matrix per IDEA, not per audience: (1) do they have a problem but not know the cause? (2) know the cause but no fix? (3) know the fix but can't apply it? (4) know fixes but can't choose? First yes = the awareness level the hook must speak to. A hook pitched above the audience's awareness is dead no matter how good the video ("detox glyphosate with these 3 herbs" fails on an audience that doesn't know what glyphosate is; move down the ladder to the symptom or the problem).
- **C: Clarity of outcome.** The payoff of watching must be obvious in the first seconds. Two directions (toward pleasure / away from pain), four mechanisms, pick exactly one: information gap (you're missing something relevant), desired result, undesired result (the mistake keeping you stuck), or A to B (from undesired state to desired state).
- **U: Unique (multiplier).** No formula can exist, by definition. Two working methods: unique power words (commandments.md; stale power words like "secret" and "ultimate" read as banner ads the brain auto-skips) and unique angle (same endpoint, unseen direction). Guardrails: don't force it, stay on-brand for who the entrepreneur is, and never sacrifice R-A-C while chasing unique.
- **B: Broaden (the exponent).** Three targeting levels: (1) the exact ideal client named in the hook: NEVER (it even disqualifies real buyers who don't self-identify with the label); (2) the niche: better, still excludes; (3) everyone who can benefit from the advice: ALWAYS. Two iron rules: always level three, and broadening the hook NEVER broadens the video; the value stays built word-for-word for the mascot. The math: 1M views at 5% ideal clients (50K) beats 10K views at 20% (2K) by 25x.

**Trust-source matching (field gospel, from expert coaching feedback on real scripts):** match the PROOF TYPE to the CONTENT TYPE before choosing any authority/credibility hook or interest peak.
- **Opinion/advice content** (the value is the creator's judgment: "the 3 sentences I close every deal with") → person-authority is required; the viewer must believe YOU before your opinion has value.
- **Demonstration content** (the viewer watches a mechanism work: a skill, a tool, a workflow) → person-authority is optional and often wasted seconds; the mechanism's self-evidence does the persuading. If proof is needed, use ARTIFACT PROOF: a result the mechanism itself produced ("this landing page did $X last month; Claude wrote it with this one skill" [verify]).
Opening a demo video with a resume is a category error; opening an opinion video without credibility is one too.

**The shtick-talk rule (Sin 3):** never borrow a hook without naming why it hit. The words are almost never the reason ("lose 30 pounds in 60 days" is the pattern everyone runs). Once you know why it worked, make it yours with one of three moves, in ascending strength:
1. **Unique presentation:** who says it, how it's said, how it's shown. (7'2" Cole doing "how to squat for tall lifters": the WHO is the unique.)
2. **Unique wording:** drop in a unique power word. ("Disgustingly well spoken," 6M views, where everyone else says "extremely." "Melt off body fat," 1.2M.)
3. **Unique angle (strongest, most common in top performers):** reach the same point from a direction nobody saw coming. (Baking soda as "the cheapest medicine they want you to think is just for baking." Opening with a statement you then call completely wrong.)

**Hook quality checks:**
- Names or clearly implies a narrow problem the mascot knows they have (Sin 1 compliance)
- **Casts the widest audience net the problem allows.** Narrow the problem, never the audience, applies to the hook hardest of all: an audience callout in the hook ("business owners," "anyone in your company") pays a skip-rate tax on everyone else and caps reach. Go broad in the hook, plant the positioning flag in the interest peak (credibility line names the who), and narrow in the content. Only call out the audience in the hook when the audience IS the unique angle (7'2" Cole: "how to squat for tall lifters").
- Contains at least one unique power word or a genuinely unique angle
- Written in You Format, with conviction (no hedges)
- Passes the smell test: does it sound like everyone else in the niche? Rewrite.

## Part 2: The visual hook system

**Definition:** a visual hook is the visual representation of the verbal hook. Used on almost every video (official rule is loose: use one when you think it's a good idea).

**Three types:**

### Type 1: Basic visual hook (text on screen, built in edit)
Formula: summarize the verbal hook into **3-7 on-screen words, at least one a power word**. The creator writes the copy.
- "I'm gonna teach you how to lose 30 pounds in 30 seconds" → on-screen: "Lose 30 pounds in 30 seconds" (power word: lose)
- "Ask these three questions and you'll never hear I can't afford it..." → "Eliminate all objections with three questions" (power word: eliminate)
- "This underground website will pay you $1,000 a day" → "Make $1,000 with secret website" (power word: secret)

### Type 2: Graphic visual hook (text + graphic, built in edit)
Devin's workhorse (~80% of his videos). **The SPG framework:**
- **S**ummarize: verbal hook in 3-7 keywords
- **P**ower word: at least one
- **G**raphic: at least one, of exactly four types:
  1. **Preview of value:** tease what the viewer will learn without giving it away ("Algorithm explained in 30 seconds" with the lesson previewed behind him, 971K views; "Never say these three words" with the three words hidden, 284K)
  2. **Borrowed interest:** something more popular than you (Hormozi's "lifetime friend" hook borrowing the sitcom Friends, 1.5M views; Leonardo DiCaprio in a sales hook; Warren Buffett in a side-hustle hook)
  3. **Symbolic image:** one image directly representing the core idea (guarantee shield for a money-back-guarantee video; slot machine for "going viral is not luck," 594K)
  4. **Transformation:** the before (undesired state: "Stop using profile pictures like this" showing the bad PFPs, 567K), the after (desired state: the jacked physique, 1.4M), or before-to-after (0 → 10K followers; follower → customer)

### Type 3: Dynamic visual hook (built in the RECORDING, not the edit)
The strongest and hardest type. Relies purely on motion and visual elements in the footage. Sometimes has S and P; NEVER has G. Same four element types with two changes:
- Borrowed interest widens to "more popular OR MORE ENGAGING than yourself" (includes the combination-hook trend: a viral clip playing before transitioning into your footage counts)
- Symbolic image becomes a physical **visual symbol** on camera (Mark Tilbury holding a clock for "time doesn't exist"; a briefcase of cash)
- Transformation examples: acting out waking up exhausted (before), the finished dessert (after), bad body language then good body language (before-to-after)
- **The visual pacifier:** the one sanctioned exception. An engaging visual (pouring tea) that does NOT represent the verbal hook, included purely because it holds attention.
- Study reference: Dr. Julie (Instagram) as the model dynamic-hook creator.

## Division of labor (workflow rule)
The creator writes the visual hook copy (S and P) for basic and graphic hooks in the script's visual hook field, ABOVE the verbal hook, before the script ships to the editor. Reason: the creator knows the audience; and if the copy changes after the editor builds a graphic around it, the graphic gets rebuilt too. Dynamic hooks need no pre-written copy: the creator performs them on camera, so scripts using a dynamic hook should include a recording direction instead (what to do/show while saying the hook).

## Skill output requirement
Every optimized script includes:
1. The verbal hook (labeled, with the uniqueness move named: presentation, wording, or angle)
2. A visual hook recommendation: type (basic / graphic / dynamic), the 3-7 word on-screen copy with the power word flagged (for basic/graphic), the graphic type and concept (for graphic), or the recording direction (for dynamic)
