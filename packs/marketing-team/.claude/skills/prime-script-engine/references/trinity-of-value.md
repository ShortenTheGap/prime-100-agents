# Trinity of Value (The Idea Gate)

Devin's most important model. Run it BEFORE writing or optimizing any script. A video is valuable only if it passes three stacked tests, in order, judged through the eyes of the mascot (see the mascot doc), never "in general." The viewer's only question is "is this worth my time?" You are raising both actual value and perceived value.

Scope: this model is for EDUCATIONAL content. A video can fail it and still perform as entertainment, but then you're playing a different game and should know it.

## The pyramid, in order of importance

### Level 1 (foundation): Clear Solution
The video is a bridge from A (the problem the viewer has now) to B (the result they want). Two requirements:
- **Simple path.** The steps from A to B are easy to follow.
- **All-encompassing.** ZERO homework after watching. No googling, no extra research, no unanswered steps. Watch, do, arrive at B.

Showing the difference between A and B is a comparison, not a solution. The bridge is the solution.
**Exit test:** could the mascot act immediately with no extra research and no unanswered questions?

### Level 2: Unique
The clear solution must beat the solution already in the viewer's head in at least ONE of three ways:
- **Easier**
- **More effective**
- **Better explained** (a fresh analogy that makes an old concept finally click counts; no new information required)

Uniqueness is measured against what YOUR mascot has already seen, not against whether the idea exists somewhere. Everyone has seen the breakfast burrito version; almost nobody has seen the quesadilla version. Pull inspiration from OUTSIDE short-form (books, studies, client experience, primary sources) to escape saturation.

**The efficiency equation:** unique does NOT require easier. Efficiency = ease + effectiveness. A solution that adds steps still passes if the added effort buys outsized results ("a little bit harder for significantly more payoff"). Judge with the mascot's resources in mind.

### Level 3: Viewer Depictable
Three sub-checks, all against the mascot:
1. Can they picture THEMSELVES doing the steps?
2. Do they BELIEVE the steps will work?
3. Is the result WORTH the effort?

For the mascot specifically, apply the two-path test in the mascot doc: he passes if he can do it himself in under an hour with hand-holding, OR hand it to his team knowing what to ask for. Videos requiring the mascot to trust his own unaided technical judgment fail even when objectively simple.

Note the asymmetry Devin flags: unique-without-depictable still gets views (the jailbroken iPad video), it just doesn't create value or clients. Ranked: clear solution, then unique, then depictable.

## Failure patterns (cross-reference scripting-sins.md, Sin 2)
- **What without how** = no clear solution (comparison masquerading as a bridge)
- **A reminder, not a video** = not unique (mascot already holds this solution)
- **Entertainment calling itself education** = not depictable (save-worthy, not do-able)

## Worked verdicts (from the course, all real videos)
- Brian Mark mindset-contrast video, 14K views: FAILS level 1. Inspiration, no bridge.
- Bulking vs. cutting meals video: FAILS level 2. Anyone who knows the words already knows the content.
- "Mr. Bradley" Jordans/jailbroken-iPad outreach: FAILS level 3. Wildly unique, nobody will do it. Still performed, because uniqueness alone gets views.
- Jeff Nippard seat-back leg extension, 7.2M views: PASSES all three. Same machine the viewer already uses, one seat adjustment, backed by a study (170% more rectus femoris growth), zero extra effort. The most potent shape: hyper-specific fix that the entire audience shares.
- Hormozi wallet-objection video: PASSES. Two concrete counters ("show me your ID," banking-app ACH), doable on the next call, obviously worth it.


## The five marks and the $5 test (quality bar on every idea)
Score all five AS THE MASCOT, never as the entrepreneur: (1) relevant to a problem the mascot actually has, (2) clear solution A to B, (3) viewer-depictable, (4) unique vs what's already in their head, (5) worth more than $5. Marks 2-4 are the Trinity; 1 and 5 wrap it.
**The $5 test:** would the mascot feel genuinely good paying five bucks for this video? Neutral = fine, nothing more. Run it as the mascot; the entrepreneur would never pay $5 for what they already know, so their own read is useless here.
**The stakes (negative reinforcement):** a strong hook on a weak video is worse than no video. It wastes the viewer's time and trains them to skip the account. This is why the gate kills instead of polishing.
