# The Eight Scripting Commandments - Rules-Only Reference (Prime)

Devin's claim: every viral script he wrote lived and died by eight commandments, reverse engineered from two years of viral scripts and distilled over three months with the Prodigy, who teaches the modules.

**Numbering.** The handouts explicitly number: 2 = Unique Power Words, 3 = As Simple As Possible, 4 = As Short As Possible, 6 = Script With Conviction. ## 1. You Format

**Rule:** make every line feel said to one person about their stuff. Two moves: replace the plural (any group word: people, they, everyone, business owners, "you guys" becomes you) and give the ownership (add your: the business becomes your business).

**Test:** scan each line for group words and ownerless nouns. If a "you" or "your" fits without breaking meaning, it belongs there. There is no faster way to improve a script than fixing missed You Format.

**Worked example:** "Whenever sales reps get told I need to think about it, if they say this, they'll immediately lose the sale" becomes "Whenever YOU get told I need to think about it, if you say this, you'll immediately lose YOUR sale." (Proof: identical Facebook ads, "save money" vs "save your money." The second: 20% more engagement.)

## 2. Unique Power Words

**Rule:** swap expected words for charged, unexpected ones. A unique power word is a power word used in a unique context that triggers attention. The surprise is the mechanism. Common power words (stop, secret, hack, stock curse words) are worn-out patterns.

**Test:** three uniqueness checks: unexpected for the sentence (most important), unexpected for the niche, unexpected for you as a person. Then the force check: if it doesn't sound like you dialed up 10-15%, cut it. The audience feels forced words.

**Worked example:** "That way you quickly get more respect from your prospect" becomes "That way you INSTANTLY COMMAND respect from your prospect." Also: "impregnate you with some viral videos," "disgustingly well spoken," "melt off body fat," bank account "GIRTHY" (only if you'd say girthy).

## 3. As Simple As Possible

**Rule:** script at Grade 5 reading level or lower. Two levers: simpler words, and shorter sentences (one point per line). Never sacrifice clarity for grade: a bad explanation explained simply is still bad. Stuck at grade 6 for clarity's sake? Stay there.

**Test:** paste the script into Hemingway Editor; the grade shows top right. Grade 5 or below passes. (54% of Americans can't understand information above fifth grade level.)

**Worked example:** Grade 11: "You'll get exponentially higher viewership by evoking emotion with your content" becomes Grade 4: "You'll get a lot more views by creating emotion with your content." (Proof: two fitness videos, same information; the one 11 reading grades lower got 6M views vs 4,000.)

## 4. As Short As Possible

**Rule:** maximize value per second. As short as possible and no shorter: a 90-second video beats a 15-second one if its value per second is higher. Cut the two culprits, over-description and redundancy, but never cut trust (credibility lines), useful context, or processing time.

**Test:** time the script in Vidyard Script Timer, then color every line: green (raises VPS, keep), yellow (neutral but deserves its place, edit here), red (lowers VPS, cut), orange markup for over-description inside a line. Tiebreak: "what happens if I remove this?" Target roughly 20% off the first draft; early on, 50% is common.

**Worked example:** 12 seconds to 6: "When you consistently create valuable content that solves the problems that your audience really cares about, you will be able to build the level of trust that they have in you as an expert" becomes "Consistently creating valuable content that solves your audience's problems builds trust in you as an expert."

## 5. Visual in Mind

**Rule:** plan visuals while scripting, not in the edit. Three keys: give visual direction (line-by-line comments, sketches, reference assets plus a short Loom; whole-video visual models go at the top of the script), show over tell (move minor-but-valuable details into visuals: the brand on screen instead of a spoken recommendation), and allow processing time (a two-second line gives your editor two seconds; pad instruction sequences with small guiding phrases so the visuals can breathe).

**Test:** per line: does the editor know what's on screen here? Could a visual carry any spoken minor detail? Do step-by-step sections give each visual enough seconds?

**Worked example:** rushed: "Go to your profile, then click settings and find media quality, then adjust these settings." Fixed: "On Instagram, go to your profile, and up the top right, open up your settings. Then scroll down until you find media quality. Then make sure your settings are toggled to look like this." (Proof: paired-visual videos average 200% more engagement, 60% more completions, 650% more memorable.)

## 6. Script With Conviction

**Rule:** two keys: definitive words (kill might/can/could/maybe/probably; use will/definitely/certainly) and concrete promises (a definitive outcome: a specific floor, never a range; and zero qualifiers: no "as long as" conditions). A smaller definitive number beats a bigger range. The only allowed disclaimer is the damaging admission: disclaimer, BUT, concrete promise. Active voice throughout.

**Test:** zero hedge words, zero qualifiers, specific floors. For any caveat: does it add a condition that must be true for the promise to hold? Qualifier, cut it. Does it just admit a negative openly? Damaging admission, it builds trust.

**Worked example:** "You'll increase your returns by about 10% or even up to 15%, as long as the market doesn't suddenly dip" becomes "You'll increase your returns by at least 10%." (Proof: doctor one "might cure you in four, five, maybe six treatments, if nothing unexpected happens" vs doctor two "this protocol will cure you after four treatments." Everyone picks doctor two.)

## 7. Audible Flow Check

**Rule:** a script is not done when it's written. It's done when every line has been read out loud, in the exact tonality you'd record with. In your head doesn't count. After any change, re-check what changed (one line: that line plus the next two; restructure: the whole script) until it passes clean.

**Test:** the read-aloud itself is the test. Expect to find three mistakes you'd have sworn weren't there, a line to cut or rework, sometimes a whole section to restructure.

**Worked example:** the failure mode it prevents: hitting record on a "finished" script, stumbling, rewriting mid-session, and turning a 10-minute recording into 30 minutes of worse video. Devin runs this on every script; so does the Prodigy on everything he touches.

## 8. Optimal Ordering (Linear + Sandwich Storytelling)

**Rule, part one (ordered information):** when information has a required sequence, use linear storytelling: a clear sequential structure early ("in three steps" tells type and count, buying viewer safety and predictability), consistent sequential markers (never switch from steps to weeks mid-video), and transitional phrases at every hand-off.

**Rule, part two (unordered information):** when tips have no required order, sandwich them: best tip FIRST (the gold nugget that makes viewers commit), second-best LAST (peak-end memory: the ending is the lasting impression that drives shares, reputation, and CTA conversion), the rest best-to-worst in the middle. Not a license for filler: three tips should be three great tips.

**Test:** ordered content: can the viewer say what step they're on at any moment, and how many remain? Unordered content: is the most valuable (or most surprising) item first and the runner-up right before the CTA?

**Worked example:** linear done right, 6M+ views: "I'm going to show you how to time box with three simple steps. The first step is called brain dumping... we then identify the top three tasks... and finally, we time box." Sandwich logic: the prospector who finds gold in his first pan keeps digging all day; find it again at day's end and he thinks the river is connected to a gold mine, so he visits your profile looking for more.

---

**The workflow the commandments imply:** write the draft in You Format with conviction, ordered optimally and worded with unique power words. Then edit: Hemingway to grade 5, Vidyard plus the color test for value per second, visual direction in the margins. Then the gate: no script records until it passes the audible flow check.
