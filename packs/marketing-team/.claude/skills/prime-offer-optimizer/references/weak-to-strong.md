# Weak to strong, by structure

Patterns, described by what was missing and what was added. There is no example copy here on purpose: a sample sentence becomes the member's sentence, and the offer has to sound like them.

Use these to recognize a shape, not to produce words.

## 1. Deliverables standing in for a promise

- **Was:** a list of what the buyer receives, with the count of sessions as the headline.
- **Missing:** any statement of what changes for the buyer.
- **Added:** one concrete outcome, with the deliverables moved below it as what makes the outcome happen.
- **Watch for:** the outcome quietly becoming a bigger claim than the proof supports.

## 2. A buyer made "specific" with demographics

- **Was:** a target described by industry, company size, and city.
- **Missing:** the problem, the ability to pay, and the conditions needed to benefit.
- **Added:** the three real qualifiers, with the demographics kept only where they changed one of them.
- **Watch for:** the owner feeling the offer got broader. It got narrower where it counts.

## 3. A delivery format sold as a process

- **Was:** a named method whose description listed calls, a portal, and a community.
- **Missing:** how any of it produces the result.
- **Added:** the steps and the link from each step to the outcome, written before the name went back on.
- **Watch for:** a name that survives the exercise unchanged. It usually has to change too.

## 4. Proof that does not match the promise

- **Was:** testimonials praising the owner's responsiveness.
- **Missing:** evidence about the outcome the offer promises.
- **Added:** the two client results that spoke to the actual promise, with starting point and timeframe kept, plus a debrief plan for the gap.
- **Watch for:** one strong result being used as the typical case.

## 5. A guarantee bolted on to make the offer feel safer

- **Was:** a money back line with no period, no responsibilities, and no remedy.
- **Missing:** four of the five parts, and any estimate of what honoring it would cost.
- **Added:** either the complete guarantee with its cost modelled, or no guarantee and a stronger proof section instead.
- **Watch for:** conditions written to make claims hard. Those are worse than no guarantee.

## 6. Several markets in one offer

- **Was:** one page addressing three buyer types with three different problems.
- **Missing:** a choice.
- **Added:** a primary offer for one buyer, with the others parked as separate versions to build later.
- **Watch for:** the owner's fear of turning away revenue. Name it, then show that the merged version speaks to nobody.

## 7. A low price with heavy custom delivery

- **Was:** an accessible price, with the owner doing bespoke work for every client.
- **Missing:** any model of hours at ten and twenty five clients.
- **Added:** the economics run, the client count where capacity breaks, and the handoff for a scale plan. The offer copy itself barely changed.
- **Watch for:** the urge to fix it by raising the price inside the draft. That is a business change and it is the owner's decision.

## 8. A strong offer brought in for a rebuild

- **Was:** a clear service, real proof, a fair price, and tired copy.
- **Missing:** nothing structural.
- **Added:** three sentence level edits and one clearer next step. The review said the rest was fine.
- **Watch for:** inventing problems to look useful. The smallest meaningful fix is the right answer more often than it feels like it is.
