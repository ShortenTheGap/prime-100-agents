# Review rubric

The three passes that pressure test an offer. Run all three. Rate every check **clear**, **weak**, or **unknown**, with a short reason in plain words.

The 3S labels keep their exact capitals here too, because these rows are copied into the
member's Offer Review: **Specific Person**, **Specific Problem**, **Specific Process**.

- "Clear" means the evidence is there and a stranger would agree.
- "Weak" means it exists but will not hold up.
- "Unknown" means you could not tell from what you were given. Unknown is a real answer. Never guess your way to a rating.

No conversion percentages. No overall score. A score lets strength in one area hide a fatal failure in another, which is the exact mistake this rubric exists to prevent.

## Pass A: will they want it?

| Check | Clear when |
|---|---|
| Specific Person | A reader can tell in one line whether this is for them |
| Specific Problem | The problem is named in the buyer's words, not the owner's category language |
| The problem matters | It costs the buyer money, time, risk, or standing, and they already know it |
| Existing desire | The offer attaches to something they already want, not something they should want |
| Urgency | There is a real reason to act now, from the buyer's situation, not from invented scarcity |
| Ability to pay | The buyer can afford this price without an unusual decision |
| Scope fits | What is included matches the size of the problem, with nothing padded on |
| Market and delivery fit | The number of reachable buyers, the price, and the delivery effort can coexist |
| Clear promise | One concrete outcome, not a list of deliverables |
| Clear next action | One primary action, and it fits how they actually sell |

## Pass B: will they believe it?

| Check | Clear when |
|---|---|
| Specific Process | A buyer can follow how the work produces the result, without specialist terms |
| What previous approaches missed | The offer names what did not work before, fairly, without insulting the buyer |
| Relevant proof | The evidence matches this buyer and this promise |
| Supported claims | Every number, result, and name traces to a real source with permission to show it |
| Appropriate timeframe | Supplied, supported, or labeled as proposed. Never added for effect |
| Guarantee, where useful | Present only when it earns its place, and complete when present |
| Plain language | Sixth grade reading level, familiar words, short sentences |
| Internal consistency | The promise, the proof, the process, the price, and the terms tell one story |

Do not require a novel process. An offer with a clear service, strong proof, and compelling value does not need a new mechanism invented for it.

## Pass C: can we deliver it as it grows?

Run with `../scripts/delivery-economics.mjs` whenever you have enough inputs. See the "Delivery and scale check" section of SKILL.md for the rules about the numbers.

| Check | Clear when |
|---|---|
| Setup work | The work to onboard one client is known and bounded |
| Recurring work | The hours per active client per period are known and bounded |
| Custom work | How much of each engagement is bespoke, and whether that share falls over time |
| Founder dependence | The share of hours only the owner can do, and what happens to it as clients grow |
| Labor and software costs | Per client costs are known, including the owner's own time at a real rate |
| Capacity | Available hours exist for the client counts being modelled |
| Guarantee obligations | What honoring the guarantee costs, at a realistic claim rate |
| Scope boundaries | What is not included is written down somewhere the buyer sees |
| Repeatability | Someone other than the owner could deliver this from what exists today |
| Acquisition economics | Cost to acquire a client, when it is known, reported separately |

## Reporting

Show the three highest impact problems first. For each one:

1. **The problem.** One sentence.
2. **Why it matters.** What it costs the business or the buyer.
3. **The recommended change.** Specific enough to act on.
4. **The trade off.** What gets worse, or what it costs, if they make the change.
5. **What is still needed.** The evidence to collect or the decision the owner has to make.

Then the rest of the findings, grouped by pass. Then the scale problems with the handoff. Then any conflict with MOAT. Then the decisions you need from the owner. Then the recommended next skills: MOAT first, Buyer Runway after.

Write the whole report to `Avatar/Offer Review.md` using `../assets/offer-review-template.md`.
