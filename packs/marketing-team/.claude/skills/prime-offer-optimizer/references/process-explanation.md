# The process explanation exercise

This produces the Process element. Run it with the owner before anything gets named.

## The exercise

Fill this in, in the owner's words, one blank at a time:

> "Most people trying to get **this result** make **this mistake**.
>
> 1. That mistake causes **this problem**.
> 2. The problem leads to **this unwanted result**.
> 3. Instead, we use **this approach**.
> 4. That helps you get **this direct benefit**.
> 5. Which means you can finally get **this larger benefit**."

When MOAT exists, use its problem stack and its enemy stack to inform the mistake and the unwanted result. Those are the mistakes and outcomes the market already told you about.

## Check the logic before you keep it

- Is this a real mistake, and a common one? Or is it one person's story?
- Does that mistake plausibly cause the stated problem?
- Does the proposed approach address the cause, or only the symptom?
- Does the direct benefit actually support the larger benefit, or is there a leap?
- Is the explanation grounded in evidence or in the owner's real experience?
- Can a buyer understand all of it without specialist terms?

Any no is worth one more question to the owner. A chain that breaks in the middle will break in front of a buyer too.

## Rules

- Write the explanation before naming the process. A name attached to an unclear explanation hides the problem instead of fixing it.
- Do not claim that every alternative fails. Say what this approach does differently and leave it there.
- Do not blame or insult the buyer for what they tried before. The mistake is a mistake most people make, which is why it is worth naming.
- If the process is generic after the exercise, say exactly what is still unclear. Do not disguise a generic process with branded language.
- Keep the filled in exercise in `Avatar/Offer Working Notes.md`. It is supporting work, not buyer facing copy, and the offer doc states it in the owner's voice rather than pasting the frame.
