# Copy Blocks — Definitions and Extraction Guide

Copy Blocks are the essential components that every piece of effective copy must contain to persuade and convert. They are the raw ingredients that power headlines, sales letters, emails, ads, and every other form of marketing copy. There are 5 key Copy Blocks.

## The 5 Copy Blocks

### 1. Pain Points

**Definition:** The current problematic state that the audience wants to escape from. The issues that keep them up at night. These are not abstract problems — they are the lived, felt experiences of the struggle.

**What to look for when extracting:**
- Specific frustrations and daily struggles
- Emotional states caused by the problem (shame, fear, anxiety, anger, exhaustion)
- How the problem shows up in their relationships, work, health, or finances
- "Problem tokens" — symptoms described the way the prospect actually experiences them
- The cost of the problem (financial, emotional, social, time)

**Examples across niches:**
- Weight Loss → Stubborn fat and feeling self-conscious
- Copywriting → Struggling to land clients
- Men's Dating → Lack of confidence and struggling to attract women
- Pet Probiotic → Worry about pet's health and digestive problems
- Skincare → Increasing wrinkles and feeling older

### 2. Promises

**Definition:** What people desire and crave. The ideal state they want to achieve. The transformation from who they are now to who they want to become.

**What to look for when extracting:**
- Specific, desirable outcomes (not vague aspirations)
- The identity shift — who they become after the transformation
- Financial, emotional, social, and lifestyle improvements
- How others will view/treat them differently
- Quick wins and long-term results

**Examples across niches:**
- Weight Loss → Achieve a slim, toned physique
- Copywriting → Attract high-paying clients consistently
- Men's Dating → Gain confidence in approaching women
- Pet Probiotic → Fix your dog's gut health
- Skincare → Achieve radiant, youthful-looking skin

### 3. Proof

**Definition:** The reasons why readers should believe the claims. Evidence that the promised transformation is real and achievable for them.

**What to look for when extracting:**
- Testimonials and client success stories
- Case studies with specific results (numbers, timeframes)
- Before/after transformations
- Expert endorsements or credibility markers
- Studies, data, or research that supports claims
- Media mentions, awards, or certifications
- Social proof (number of customers, community size)
- Demonstrations that show the method working
- The creator's own results and track record

### 4. Constraints

**Definition:** The objections and mental barriers that prevent people from buying. These are the reasons prospects talk themselves out of taking action, even when they want the result.

**What to look for when extracting:**
- **Identity constraints:** "I'm not the kind of person who..." / "I'm too old/young/inexperienced"
- **Values constraints:** Conflicts with personal principles or what they believe is right
- **Belief constraints (internal):** "I've tried everything, nothing works for me" / "I'm not capable"
- **Belief constraints (external):** "All solutions are scams" / "The industry just wants my money"
- **Resource constraints:** Not enough time, money, energy, knowledge, or support
- **Experience constraints:** Past failures that make them skeptical ("I always cheat on my diet" / "Clients always ghost me")

### 5. Curiosity (Mechanism)

**Definition:** The unique, novel solution or "secret" that bridges the gap between the reader's current pain points and the promised desires. This is the hook — the thing that makes this offer different from everything they've tried before. It should be positioned as new, unique, and unlike anything else in the market.

**What to look for when extracting:**
- Named systems, methods, or frameworks
- Unique mechanisms or processes that explain HOW the solution works
- Hidden causes or newly discovered explanations for the problem
- Surprising or counterintuitive approaches
- Proprietary tools, technology, or intellectual property
- Origin stories (where the method came from — especially if exotic or unexpected)
- Anything that can be "caricatured" into a curiosity-inducing hook

**Examples across niches:**
- Weight Loss → The "Ozempic Killer"
- Copywriting → "Invisible Outreach" Method
- Men's Dating → "High-Achiever's Paradox"
- Pet Probiotic → "Dinner Time Trick"
- Skincare → "Ageless Glow" Activator

## Extraction Rules

1. **Pull ALL relevant Copy Blocks exactly as written** — Use the source material's own language. Do not paraphrase or soften. The user's words are the raw material.
2. **Identify up to 5 key insights per block** — Focus on the most powerful, persuasive elements. Quality over quantity.
3. **Maintain accuracy and specificity** — Vague copy blocks are useless. "Wants to make more money" is weak. "Wants to hit $100K/month so she can homeschool her son and buy a bigger home" is strong.
4. **Provide only the structured output** — No commentary, no analysis, no suggestions. Just the formatted copy blocks.
5. **If a block is thin in the source material** — Extract what's available. If the source has no proof elements, note it so the user knows to provide them.

## How Copy Blocks Get Used

These extracted blocks become the inputs for:
- **Teaser emails** — Pain + Promise + Curiosity drive the itch; Proof builds belief; Constraints get dismissed
- **Video ad scripts** — All 5 blocks power the script with 90-95% copy velocity
- **Sales pages** — Each block maps to sections of the page
- **Social media posts** — Individual blocks become standalone post angles
- **Headlines and hooks** — Curiosity + Promise or Pain + Curiosity combinations

The better the extraction, the better everything downstream performs.
