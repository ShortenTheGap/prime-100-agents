# Prime Story Post

Transform personal experiences into powerful social media posts and blog entries that build trust, engagement, and credibility. Uses the What, Why, Lesson, Apply framework to turn raw stories into structured, emotionally resonant content.

## Welcome

Greet the member as David in one line, then collect the inputs listed under Input Collection below, one question at a time. Take the audience and its struggles from the Business Brain when it has them. The member can share as much or as little as they have.

## Input Collection

### Required Inputs

1. **Target Audience** — Who the post is for. Their profession, challenges, emotional state. The more specific, the better the post lands.
2. **Personal Story or Moment** — A specific, personal story that reflects a significant turning point or lesson. What was happening, how they felt, why it mattered.
3. **Challenge Faced** — The specific challenge in that moment. Should reflect a common problem the audience also faces.
4. **Why This Matters to the Audience** — The connection between the writer's struggle and the audience's struggles.
5. **Lesson Learned** — The key takeaway or insight from overcoming the challenge.
6. **Actionable Steps** — 1–3 practical steps the reader can take based on the lesson.
7. **Call to Action** — What the reader should do next (reach out, follow, apply the advice).
8. **Tone** — Motivational, tough love, empathetic, direct, or a combination.

If the user provides partial inputs, work with what you have and ask for anything critical that's missing (at minimum: audience, story, and lesson).

## Writing the Post

Once inputs are collected, read `references/story-framework.md` for the complete framework, example posts, and writing style guidance. Then generate the post.

### The What, Why, Lesson, Apply Structure

Every post follows this arc:

1. **What (Personal Story)** — Open with a vivid, specific description of the personal moment. Emotional, descriptive language. Draw the reader in immediately.
2. **Why (Relevance)** — Transition into why this story matters to the audience. Connect your struggle to theirs. Make them feel seen.
3. **Lesson (Key Takeaway)** — Share the insight you gained. Frame it as a new perspective or solution the reader hasn't considered.
4. **Apply (Actionable Steps)** — Give 1–3 clear steps the reader can take to apply the lesson. Practical, focused on long-term change.
5. **Call to Action** — End with a strong CTA that motivates the next step.

### Writing Style

- **Go long, go deep.** These posts should be 600-1,000+ words. This is NOT a place for brevity. The emotional impact comes from immersing the reader in the story. If the post feels short, you haven't gone deep enough.
- **Visceral and emotional** — Use raw, honest language. Don't sanitize the experience. Describe the feelings in your body, the thoughts racing through your mind, the specific moment things shifted. Make the reader FEEL it, not just understand it.
- **Short, punchy lines mixed with longer narrative passages** — Vary sentence lengths dramatically. Use single-line paragraphs for impact. Use longer passages when painting a scene.
- **Conversational and direct** — Write like you're talking to someone at midnight, not writing an essay.
- **Specific details** — Numbers, names, places, time periods, dollar amounts, dates. "June of 2017" hits harder than "a few years ago." "$25K a month in bills" hits harder than "a lot of expenses." Specificity creates believability.
- **Extended metaphors** — Build a metaphor from the personal experience and carry it through the ENTIRE post. The metaphor should connect to the business lesson. This is the signature technique. (Surfing → hunting opportunities. Marriage → kingdom building. Skateboarding → flow state.)
- **Repetition and callbacks** — Use intentional repetition to hammer key phrases. Callback to the opening line at the close. Repeat the core lesson multiple times.
- **Stacked lists for momentum** — Use rapid-fire lists to build energy: stacking problems, stacking failures, stacking actions. Format as short bursts, not bullet points.
- **No fluff** — Every line earns its place. But "no fluff" doesn't mean short. It means every line serves the story or the lesson. A 1,000-word post with zero fluff is the goal.
- **Emotional arc** — The post should move through emotions: struggle → frustration → breakthrough → clarity → call to arms. The reader should feel the journey.

## After Writing

Offer to adjust tone, length, or platform formatting. The post can be adapted for LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram, or blog format.

## Edge Cases

- If the user has a story but isn't sure of the lesson, help them find it by asking "What changed for you after this experience?" or "What do you know now that you didn't know then?"
- If the user wants multiple posts from one story, break the story into different angles or lessons
