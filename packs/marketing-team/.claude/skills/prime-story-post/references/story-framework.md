# Personal Story Content Framework — Full Reference

This file contains the complete What, Why, Lesson, Apply framework with detailed writing guidance and patterns extracted from high-performing example posts. Use this to craft emotionally resonant, authority-building story content.

## The Framework

### 1. What (Personal Story)

Open with a specific, vivid personal moment or turning point. This is the hook — it must grab attention in the first 2–3 lines.

**How to write it:**
- Start with a bold, declarative statement or metaphor that encapsulates the story's theme
- Ground the reader in a specific time, place, and situation
- Use sensory and emotional details — what you saw, felt, heard, feared
- Don't start with backstory. Start in the middle of the action or at the peak moment, then fill in context
- Be brutally honest. The rawer the story, the deeper the connection

**Patterns from top-performing posts:**
- Opening with a bold metaphorical statement: "YOU MUST LEARN TO HUNT… the waves will not just come to you."
- Opening with a provocative claim: "IF THE LEADER DOESN'T RISE, THE TEAM & THE KINGDOM DIE…"
- Opening with a simple setup and specific date: "June of 2017 I made a decision."
- Using an extended metaphor from a non-business experience (surfing, skateboarding, marriage) to illuminate a business principle

### 2. Why (Relevance to the Reader)

Transition from your story to the reader's world. This is where you make them feel like you're talking directly to them.

**How to write it:**
- Bridge your experience to their pain points explicitly
- Use "you" language — shift from "I" to "you"
- Name their specific struggles, fears, and frustrations
- Don't just say "you might relate to this." Show them exactly how your story IS their story

**Patterns from top-performing posts:**
- Direct address: "So friend. I know you want to blame the market. But consider this."
- Conditional challenge: "And if the people at home have lost faith in you? If you have lost respect for yourself? If your bank accounts are dry?"
- Universal extension: "Skateboarding, Surfing, Marriage, Parenting, Business, Marketing, Selling… They're all activities that have a science and flow to them."

### 3. Lesson (Key Takeaway)

Share the core insight — the thing you learned that changed everything. Frame it as a principle, not just a personal observation.

**How to write it:**
- State the lesson clearly and memorably — ideally in one punchy line
- Explain why this lesson was counterintuitive or hard to accept
- Show the before/after of learning this lesson in your own life
- Make the lesson feel universal — something that applies beyond your specific situation

**Patterns from top-performing posts:**
- Named principle as anchor: "AIM SMALL TO GROW BIG"
- Metaphor as lesson: "I went from someone in a restaurant ordering a meal to the one hunting the meal down out in the wild."
- Repetition for emphasis: "I stopped waiting… I started making. I stopped wishing… I started opening."
- Counterintuitive framing: "I didn't have to be the best at surfing to catch the most waves. I had to be the best at HUNTING the waves."

### 4. Apply (Actionable Steps)

Give the reader 1–3 concrete things they can do. These should be simple, specific, and focused on long-term change.

**How to write it:**
- Keep steps simple — one action per step
- Make them doable today, not "someday"
- Tie each step back to the lesson
- Frame them as the beginning of a transformation, not a quick fix

**Patterns from top-performing posts:**
- Daily discipline framing: "Master yourself. Master your marriage. Master your money. And do it every single day."
- Single imperative: "START HUNTING!"
- Permission to begin: "So let go. Stop stopping. AND GO!"

### 5. Call to Action

End with a strong close that motivates the reader to take the next step.

**How to write it:**
- Match the tone of the post (tough love posts get tough love CTAs)
- Can be an invitation to connect, a challenge to act, or a declaration
- Keep it short and punchy — don't dilute the ending

**Patterns from top-performing posts:**
- Declarative close: "The End." (used as a signature move — the story IS the lesson)
- Single-word close: "Onwards."
- Identity statement: "YOU-ARE-A-HUNTER. You were born to hunt."

## Writing Style Guide

### Length and Depth
- Target 600-1,000+ words. These are NOT short posts. The emotional impact comes from immersion.
- The "What" section (the story itself) should be the longest section — at least 40-50% of the total post. Don't rush through the story to get to the lesson. The story IS the content. Linger in the details. Let the reader live the experience with you.
- When describing a moment of struggle or breakthrough, slow down. Add more detail, not less. What did it feel like physically? What was the conversation? What were the exact words someone said? What did you see when you looked around?
- If the post feels like it could be a LinkedIn tip post, it's too short. These should feel like a chapter from a book — a complete narrative that takes the reader on a journey.

### Sentence Structure
- Mix very short lines (1–5 words) with longer narrative sentences
- Use single-line paragraphs for emphasis — let key statements breathe
- Use fragments intentionally: "The marriage. The family. The future."
- Stack parallel structures for rhythm: "Rep after rep. Round after round."

### Emotional Intensity
- Don't hold back. Use raw, unfiltered language when the moment calls for it
- Profanity is acceptable when it serves the emotional truth of the story — it signals authenticity
- Describe failure and pain as vividly as you describe success
- Vulnerability builds more trust than perfection

### Specificity
- Use exact numbers: "700 days in the water," "$10k salary a month," "17 years of marriage"
- Name specific people, places, events when possible
- Describe specific moments: "My coach slowed me down and said, 'Listen, you're going to have to learn how to Hunt.'"
- Time references ground the story: "June of 2017," "the past 7 years of our 17 year marriage"

### Extended Metaphor
- The most powerful posts build an extended metaphor from a non-business experience and carry it through to the business lesson
- Surfing → business hunting. Marriage → kingdom building. Skateboarding → flow state.
- The metaphor should feel natural, not forced — it works because the writer genuinely experienced both sides
- Bring the metaphor full circle at the end

### Repetition and Callbacks
- Use intentional repetition to hammer key phrases: "I stopped… I started… I stopped… I started…"
- Callback to the opening line at the close for a satisfying arc
- Repeat the core lesson phrase multiple times throughout (e.g., "AIM SMALL TO GROW BIG" appears 4+ times)

### Lists and Stacking
- Use rapid-fire lists to create momentum: stacking problems, stacking failures, stacking actions
- Format lists as short bursts, not bullet points — they should feel like punches, not a checklist
- "Client Fulfillment. Process management. People Leadership. Systems. Optics and Tracking."

### Tone Adaptation

**Motivational:** Uplifting, forward-looking, emphasis on possibility and potential. Uses "you can" and "you will" language.

**Tough Love:** Direct, confrontational, no-excuses. Uses "stop" and "start" language. Challenges the reader's identity.

**Empathetic:** Understanding, validating the struggle before pushing for change. "I know you want to blame the market. But consider this."

**Direct:** Cut the fluff, state the principle, show the evidence, give the steps. Minimal emotional padding.

Most powerful posts blend tones — empathetic opening, tough love middle, motivational close.
