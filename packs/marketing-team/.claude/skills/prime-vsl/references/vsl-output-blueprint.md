# VSL Script Output Blueprint

Generate the VSL script ONLY after the pre-flight check is confirmed. Use the complete ingredient summary as your source for every detail.

## Script Structure

### Section 1: Hook & Quick Proof (60–90 seconds)
- Attention-grabbing headline that speaks directly to the Person
- Promise of what they'll learn
- Rapid-fire proof using 3–5 client names with specific results from the Proof section

### Section 2: Origin Story & Credibility (60 seconds)
- Your connection to their problem (from Person section)
- Why you're qualified to help

### Section 3: Problem Deep Dive (2–3 minutes)
- Their dream vs. reality (from Problem section)
- Pointable problems with specific details
- Emotional cost — what it feels like
- Their exact metaphor for their current state

### Section 4: Shared Enemy (2–3 minutes)
- Bad advice they've been following (from Poison section)
- Why it's wrong — your critique
- "It's not your fault because..." — their exact phrase
- The metaphor for the broken system

### Section 5: Philosophy & Mission (60 seconds)
- What you believe they deserve (from Philosophy)
- Your mission statement

### Section 6: The Plan — Your Model (8–12 minutes)
This is the longest section. For each of the 3 pillars:
- Name the pillar and its benefit
- Walk through the 2–3 steps with benefits
- Tell the case study that proves this pillar works — use the actual client NAME and details from Proof

### Section 7: More Proof (1–2 minutes)
- Additional case studies using remaining names and stories from Proof
- Include emotional details

### Section 8: Call to Action (2–3 minutes)
- What happens next (from Prize)
- How to claim it
- Safety / no pressure element
- Scarcity / reason to act now
- Exact next step — crystal clear instruction

**Total target: 15–25 minutes (2,500–4,000 words)**

---

## Critical Writing Rules

1. **Use ACTUAL client names** from the Proof section — never make up new ones
2. **Reference EXACT metaphors** they gave you — don't substitute your own
3. **Use their SPECIFIC numbers, details, and descriptions** — not rounded or generalized versions
4. **Speak directly to the PERSON they described** — use their exact language
5. **Weave in emotional details they shared** — the feelings, the fears, the stakes
6. **NO generic placeholders** — every detail comes from what they told you
7. **If you're about to write something generic, STOP** — go back to the ingredient summary and find the specific detail

---

## Style & Tone

### Voice:
- Conversational — like talking to a friend over coffee
- Punchy — short sentences, varied rhythm, no fluff
- Specific — names, numbers, moments, metaphors (from THEIR answers)
- Emotional — connect to feelings, not just facts
- Confident — you know this works
- Human — contractions, personality, warmth

### Sentence craft:
- Vary sentence length dramatically
- Use fragments. Like this.
- Include rhetorical questions (sparingly)
- Use "you" and "your" constantly
- Active voice: "You'll build" not "A framework will be built"

### Words to avoid:
"delve," "unlock," "leverage," "robust," "streamline," "dive deep," "In today's fast-paced world," "Are you tired of"

### Formatting:
- Bold for emphasis on KEY phrases
- Line breaks for readability
- "..." for dramatic pauses
- ALL CAPS sparingly for emphasis

---

## Quality Check Before Delivering

Verify:
- Every client name in the script came from the Proof section
- Every metaphor came from the user's answers
- The Person description matches what they said
- The framework pillars match their Plan exactly
- The CTA matches their Prize exactly
- The script reads naturally as spoken word
- No generic filler snuck in
- Length is approximately 2,500–4,000 words
