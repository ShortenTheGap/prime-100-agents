# Short Form Script Framework — Hook-Retain-Payoff-CTA

Every script follows this exact 4-part structure. Each part has specific rules and formats.

---

## Part 1: HOOK (0–5 seconds)

The hook has THREE components that work together. All three must appear in every hook option.

### Visual Hook
A specific action or movement that happens on screen. This creates movement that catches the eye before they process words.

Examples: "Step into frame," "Sit down in chair," "Hold up notepad," "Pour coffee," "Adjust glasses," "Quick camera pan," "Walk toward camera," "Close laptop dramatically," "Pick up phone and put it down"

### Verbal Hook
The opening line they hear. Must grab attention immediately and create curiosity or highlight a problem.

**Proven hook formulas:**
- **The Contrarian:** "Stop [doing common thing]. Here's why it's killing your [result]."
- **The Bold Claim:** "This [simple thing] generates more [result] than [expensive/complex thing]."
- **The Mistake:** "The biggest mistake [audience] make with [topic] is..."
- **The If/Then:** "If you're [situation], you need to hear this."
- **The Number:** "[X] things about [topic] that nobody talks about."
- **The Question:** "Why do [audience] keep [doing wrong thing] when [better approach] exists?"
- **The Story Opener:** "I lost [something] because I made this one mistake..."
- **The Secret:** "[Successful people/companies] do this one thing differently with their [topic]."
- **The Warning:** "If you're [doing common thing], stop right now. Here's what happens..."
- **The Curiosity Gap:** "I'm about to tell you the one thing that changed how I [topic] forever."
- **The Challenge:** "Most [audience] can't answer this question: [provocative question]"
- **The Pattern Interrupt:** Start mid-sentence or mid-action, breaking expected patterns

### Written Hook
Text that appears on screen or in caption preview. Can be on whiteboard, notepad, or as overlay. Physical text (whiteboard/notepad) performs better than digital overlays. Should reinforce or complement the verbal hook.

---

## Part 2: RETAIN (Middle ~60 seconds)

Choose ONE format. Keep to approximately 150–180 words (60 seconds when read aloud).

### Story Format
- Tell a relevant narrative with a clear point
- Include specific details (names, numbers, situations — not vague generalities)
- Has beginning, middle, end
- Most engaging format when you have a good story
- Keep it tight — no unnecessary backstory

### List Format
- "Here are the [3–5] things you need to know..."
- State the number upfront (creates an open loop — they'll watch to get all items)
- Put SECOND BEST point first (strong open)
- Put BEST point last (reward for watching)
- Can tease: "Make sure you catch the last one..."
- Numbered items keep people watching to completion

### Steps Format
- "Follow these [3–5] steps to achieve [result]..."
- Number each step clearly
- Make each step actionable and specific
- State total number upfront (open loop)
- Each step should be concise — one sentence of explanation max

---

## Part 3: PAYOFF (5–10 seconds)

The one clear takeaway — your "bumper sticker" message.

**Rules:**
- Crystal clear, not vague or abstract
- Stated explicitly, never implied
- ONE insight they should remember (not two, not three — one)
- Should pass the "$5 test" — was this worth their 90 seconds?
- 1–2 sentences maximum

**Examples:**
- "Sales aren't lost at the end. They're lost in the first 2 minutes. Get that right."
- "You don't need more leads. You need to stop losing the ones you already have."
- "The business that wins isn't the smartest. It's the one that executes fastest."

---

## Part 4: CTA (Final 3–5 seconds)

Tell them exactly what to do next. Choose ONE:

### Follow CTA
"Follow for more [type of content]"
- Simple and effective. Grows audience. Use when no specific resource.

### Resource CTA
"Comment [KEYWORD] to get my free [specific resource]"
- Generates leads directly. Starts conversations. Use when you have a relevant lead magnet.

### Altruistic CTA
"Send this to a [specific person] who needs to hear this"
- Increases reach through shares. Lower resistance (helping others, not you).
- Example: "Send this to another business owner dealing with this. They might not listen to me, but they'll listen to you."

## Writing Style

- Conversational and natural — like talking to a friend who's also a client
- Confident but not arrogant
- Specific, not vague — details make it real
- Action-oriented — focus on what they can DO
- Benefit-focused — what's in it for the viewer
- Energetic but authentic — enthusiasm without hype
- No corporate jargon or buzzwords
- Write how people actually talk, not how they write
