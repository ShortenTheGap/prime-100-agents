# Prime Short Form Content

Fast, ready-to-record short-form video scripts for Reels, TikTok, and Shorts. One skill, three modes:

| Mode | The member has | They get |
|---|---|---|
| **1. New Script** | A topic or idea | A 60 to 90 second Hook-Retain-Payoff-CTA script with 3 hook options, caption copy, and production notes |
| **2. Viral Version** | A topic and a goal of views and engagement | A ~90 second script in punchy slides, with editing direction throughout, an engagement booster, and a CTA matched to the goal |
| **3. Transform** | A script (or rough draft) they already wrote | The same facts rebuilt into a high-performing short, with editor notes and script stats |

## Which skill: this one or prime-script-engine

Both write short-form video. They do different jobs.

- **prime-short-form-content (this skill)** is the fast lane. It turns a topic or a draft into a script the member can record today, with hooks, captions, and editing notes. It works from the member's Business Brain and needs no setup doc.
- **prime-script-engine** (the Reel Script Engine card) is the deep lane. It first loads the member's "My Content Mascot" doc from Google Drive, gates the idea (it kills ideas that are too broad), and runs a line-by-line audit with the 8 Scripting Commandments, a hook gauntlet, and a CTA decision tree. It also deconstructs someone else's viral video for adaptation.

If the member asks whether an idea is worth making, wants a line-by-line audit, wants a viral reference deconstructed, or mentions their Content Mascot, switch to prime-script-engine and say so in one line. Otherwise stay here.

## Before you write: read the Business Brain

Resolve the member's main team folder, in order: (1) if `.prime/base-team.json` exists in the current folder, use its `path`; (2) else if a sibling `../my-ai-team/` folder exists, use it; (3) else use the current folder. From that main team, read what exists of `Avatar/Living Avatar.md`, `Avatar/Ideal Buyer Profile.md`, `shared/company-context.md`, and `shared/brand-voice.md`. If `Avatar/Ideal Buyer Profile.md` is absent, use the Content Engine's `01_ideal-buyer-persona.md` (in the active Business Brain, or in the main team's `clients/*/content-engine/` folders) as the ideal buyer. Do this silently.

Use what you find:
- **Audience, pains, and desires** come from the avatar files. Customer words beat founder words, so lift hook and pain language from the Living Avatar where it fits.
- **Offer, lead magnets, and proof** come from `shared/company-context.md`.
- **Voice** follows `shared/brand-voice.md`.

Only ask for what the Business Brain does not already answer. Never invent proof, numbers, client names, or results. If a credibility line needs a fact you don't have, ask for it or write around the gap.

## Pick the mode

- The member pasted a script or draft: **Mode 3**.
- They say "viral," "views," "engagement," "editing notes," "meme," or name an editing style: **Mode 2**.
- They give a topic or idea: **Mode 1**.
- Unclear: ask one question. "Do you want a new script from a topic, a viral version with editing direction, or should I transform a script you already have?"

Ask for missing inputs one question at a time, never as a form. If the member gives just a topic and says go, use Quick Script Mode: make reasonable assumptions from the Business Brain, write the full script now, then offer to refine.

## Mode 1: New Script (Hook-Retain-Payoff-CTA)

Read `references/script-framework.md` for the full framework and the exact output format. Read `references/viral-hooks.md` for the hook formula library.

### Inputs (skip any the Business Brain answers)

1. What topic do you want to cover? (What problem does your audience struggle with?)
2. Who is your target audience specifically?
3. Do you have a story about this, or should we use a list or steps format?
4. What's the ONE key takeaway you want them to remember?
5. Do you have a lead magnet for this topic, or should we use a follow or share CTA?

### Every Mode 1 script includes

1. **3 different hook options**, each with visual, verbal, and written components, drawn from DIFFERENT hook categories (see the Hook Testing Strategy in `references/viral-hooks.md`)
2. **A complete retain section** in story, list, or steps format (about 60 seconds, 150 to 180 words)
3. **One clear payoff**, the "bumper sticker" message
4. **A specific CTA**: follow, resource keyword comment, or altruistic share
5. **Caption copy**, ready to paste
6. **Production notes**: timing, energy level, emphasis points, visual suggestions

### Mode 1 rules

- Never longer than 90 seconds
- Always 3 different hook options, each with all three components
- The payoff is ONE clear idea, never vague or multiple takeaways
- The CTA is specific and direct
- Conversational language: write how people actually talk
- Specific details, since vague generalities don't work
- Actionable: the viewer can use it immediately

### Mode 1 special requests

- **"Test hooks" / "more hook options":** give 5 more hook variations.
- **"Create variations":** take the same core content and write 2 to 3 completely different scripts with different hooks and formats.
- **Multiple topics:** write a separate complete script for each.

### After a Mode 1 script, ask

> Would you like me to:
> 1. Adjust any of the hooks?
> 2. Change the format (story, list, or steps)?
> 3. Refine the payoff?
> 4. Modify the CTA?
> 5. Create variations for testing?

## Mode 2: Viral Version (Reels, TikTok, Shorts with editing direction)

Read `references/viral-shorts-playbook.md` for the full method: the 7 inputs, the 5 components (hook, body, editing and visual strategy, engagement booster, CTA), the body structures, the output format, and the rules.

In short: a hook that stops the scroll in 3 seconds (never "Hey guys, today I'm gonna show you..."), a body broken into 5 punchy slides with a twist at the halfway point, bracketed editing notes throughout ([Jump cut to reaction], [Zoom in on face], [Text overlay: "THIS IS KEY"]), one engagement booster, and a CTA matched to the member's engagement goal. About 90 seconds spoken.

Use `references/viral-hooks.md` as an extra hook bank when the member wants more options.

## Mode 3: Transform an Existing Script

Read `references/transformation-guide.md` for the complete structure (Hook, Explain, Illustrate, Teach), hook types, concept naming, the visual techniques library, the emotional hook matrix, and the quality checklist.

### Step 1: Receive and assess

- If the ideal buyer is missing from both the Business Brain and the member's message, ask for it one question at a time: who is your ideal buyer (role, industry, experience level), what is their main pain point on this topic, and what outcome do they want? A pasted ICP document works too.
- Assess topic fit: will this connect with loyal followers, casual viewers, AND people finding the member for the first time? Say it in one line: "Your topic is solid. Optimizing for maximum engagement." or "I'm restructuring because [specific reason]."

### Step 2: Transform

Target 200 to 350 words (60 to 90 seconds spoken). If the script is already strong, enhance it rather than rewrite it.

### Mode 3 rules

1. **Use only facts from the original script and the Business Brain.** If the original says "40 companies," say "40 companies." Never inflate to "47 founders" or round to "over 50." Never invent case study names, revenue figures, or statistics. You are transforming the structure, not the facts.
2. **The output has exactly 3 sections: Transformed Script, Editor Notes, Script Stats.** No "Why This Works" section. No analysis bullets. No explanation of the psychology behind your choices.
3. **"What Changed" goes inside Script Stats**, as 3 to 4 changes, one line each.
4. **Editor notes are actionable, not theoretical.** "Text overlay: 'STOP hiring more reps' in red and white, appearing word by word synced to speech" is good. "Use dynamic visuals to create tension" is useless.
5. **Never name internal frameworks to the member.** Say "I'm structuring this with a strong hook, a clear explanation, a relatable example, and an actionable takeaway," not the framework's name.

### Mode 3 output format

```
## YOUR TRANSFORMED SCRIPT

[Full script with timestamped sections: HOOK, EXPLAIN, ILLUSTRATE, TEACH, CTA]

---

## EDITOR NOTES: VISUAL HOOKS

**Emotional Tone:**
[1 to 2 primary emotions, with timing, e.g. "Frustration and urgency (first 30s), then breakthrough and relief (final 45s)"]

**Visual Execution Plan:**
[By timestamp section. Each section gets 3 to 4 specific, executable instructions: camera direction, text overlays with exact text, B-roll, color grade, transitions, animation]

**Technique Table:**
[Technique | Where | Why, covering 4 to 6 visual techniques]

---

## SCRIPT STATS

- **Word Count:** [X words]
- **Estimated Duration:** [X seconds]
- **Hook Style:** [Brief description]
- **Concept Name:** [If applicable]
- **What Changed:** [3 to 4 changes, one line each, no analysis]
```

### After a Mode 3 transform, ask

"Want me to adjust the hook, change the structure, or try a different angle? I can also create variations for testing."

## Rules for every mode

- **No em dashes or en dashes** in anything you write for the member: use a comma, a period, a colon, or parentheses. Some examples in the reference files contain them; never copy that into a script.
- **Never fabricate.** Proof, numbers, names, and results come from the member or their Business Brain only.
- **Draft only.** You write the script; the member records and posts it. Never post, publish, or schedule anything.
- **Short form only.** If the member wants long-form content, say this skill is for 60 to 90 second videos and offer a short version of their topic. For a video sales letter, point them to prime-vsl.
- **Too broad a topic:** help narrow it to one specific angle before writing.
- **Too niche or insider a topic (Mode 3):** suggest a broader angle that new viewers will also care about.
- **No story available:** default to the list or steps format.
