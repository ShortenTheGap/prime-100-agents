# Prime Expert Writing Guidelines

These are the authoritative rules for refining text. Apply every rule to every piece of writing. A key symptom of mastery is being able to take the complex and make it incredibly simple.

---

## Rule 1: Keep Everything Below 3rd Grade Reading Level

Use the simplest words possible. If a simpler word exists, use it. Write so anyone can understand on the first read.

- Before: "We utilize leverage to gain higher returns on our equity checks."
- After: "We borrow money so we can buy bigger stuff (and make more) than we could with our cash alone."

Replace jargon, technical terms, and fancy vocabulary with plain language unless the audience specifically requires technical terminology.

---

## Rule 2: Present Voice

Use present tense constructions. Remove progressive/continuous forms when present simple works.

- Before: "When you're creating your sales page."
- After: "When you create your sales page."

The present voice is more direct, immediate, and confident.

---

## Rule 3: Active, Not Passive

The subject should perform the action. Passive voice hides the actor and weakens the sentence.

- Before: "The body was carried out of the room."
- After: "We carried the body out of the room."

Scan for "was," "were," "been," "being" + past participle. Rewrite with a clear subject doing the action.

---

## Rule 4: Avoid Adverbs (They Usually Mean Weak Verbs)

If you need an adverb, the verb is too weak. Find a stronger verb instead.

- Before: "We shut the door really hard."
- After: "We slammed the door."

Words to eliminate: very, super, way, actually, really, extremely, incredibly, absolutely, quite, rather, somewhat, fairly, pretty (as modifier).

When you spot an adverb, ask: "Is there a single verb that captures this meaning?" There almost always is.

---

## Rule 5: Simple, Short Sentences

- One comma maximum per sentence
- If a sentence has two commas, cut the middle clause or split into two sentences
- **Never use em dashes.** Not sometimes. Not occasionally. Never.

- Before: "We tried to escape but the man, with the red mask, chased us until we lost our breath."
- After: "The red-masked man chased us until we lost our breath. We were trapped."

Short sentences hit harder. They create rhythm. They build momentum. Long, winding sentences lose the reader before they reach the point.

---

## Rule 6: Positive Language

Frame statements in the affirmative. Tell the reader what TO do, not what NOT to do.

- Before: "Don't stop."
- After: "Keep going."

- Before: "Don't leave the facility."
- After: "Stay inside."

Positive language is clearer, more direct, and more motivating. The brain processes affirmative statements faster than negatives.

---

## Rule 7: Remove Redundant Words

If two phrases say the same thing, cut one.

- Before: "We simultaneously left the building at the same time."
- After: "We left the building at the same time."

Common redundancies to watch for: "end result," "free gift," "past history," "future plans," "basic fundamentals," "added bonus," "completely finished," "each and every."

---

## Rule 8: Remove Unnecessary Words

If a word can be removed without losing meaning, remove it. Every word must earn its place.

- Before: "8 Guidelines For Writing I Live By."
- After: "8 Writing Guidelines I Live By."

- Before: "He was able to get out of his car."
- After: "He exited his car."

Common unnecessary constructions:
- "was able to" → just use the verb
- "in order to" → "to"
- "due to the fact that" → "because"
- "at this point in time" → "now"
- "for the purpose of" → "to" or "for"
- "in the event that" → "if"
- "it is important to note that" → cut entirely

---

## Final Rule: No Em Dashes

Do not use em dashes in any writing output. Ever. Use periods, commas, or colons instead. Restructure the sentence if needed.

This rule has no exceptions.
