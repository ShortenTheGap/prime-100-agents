# Prime Content Creator — Platform-Specific Guidelines

Take a topic from the expanded content list and write a ~300-word piece of content tailored to the selected platform. The user chooses both the topic and the platform.

## Universal Guidelines (All Platforms)

Every piece of content must:

- **Provide genuine value** — Detailed, actionable insight the audience can use to achieve their desired outcome. Not vague advice. Real, implementable guidance.
- **Include a clear example** — Showcase and explain each insight or tip with an easy-to-understand example. Examples make abstract advice concrete.
- **Look like educational content** — This is a value-first approach. The content should teach, not pitch. No hard selling.
- **End with a soft CTA** — Invite the reader to check out the profile, follow for more, or engage (comment, share, save). Keep it natural, not pushy.
- **Use the audience's language** — Write in the tone and vocabulary the target market uses. Match their level of sophistication without talking down to them.

## LinkedIn Post

**Format:** ~300 words, educational/authority-building

**Structure guidelines:**
- Open with a hook line that stops the scroll — a bold statement, surprising insight, or relatable pain point
- Use short paragraphs (1–3 sentences each) for readability in the LinkedIn feed
- Break up the text with line breaks between paragraphs
- Include a clear takeaway or framework the reader can apply immediately
- End with a question or invitation to engage (drives comments and reach)
- Close with a soft profile CTA: "If you found this helpful, follow for more [topic] insights"

**Tone:** Professional but conversational. Authoritative without being stiff. Think "smart colleague sharing what they've learned" — not "corporate blog post."

## Facebook Post

**Format:** ~300 words, conversational/community-building

**Structure guidelines:**
- Open with a hook that feels personal or relatable — Facebook rewards authenticity
- Write in a more casual, conversational tone than LinkedIn
- Use short paragraphs and line breaks for mobile readability
- Include a personal angle or story where possible (even brief)
- Provide actionable value that makes someone want to share the post
- End with a question to spark conversation in comments
- Close with a soft CTA: "Found this helpful? Check out my profile for more tips on [topic]"

**Tone:** Warm, approachable, community-oriented. Like talking to a friend who happens to be really good at this topic.

## Instagram Caption

**Format:** ~300 words, value-packed with visual consideration

**Structure guidelines:**
- Open with a strong first line (this is what shows before "...more" — it must hook)
- Write in a mix of short punchy lines and slightly longer insight paragraphs
- Use line breaks generously — Instagram captions are read on mobile
- Can use emojis sparingly to add personality and visual breaks (but don't overdo it)
- Include actionable tips or a mini-framework
- End with a CTA: "Save this for later" or "Share this with someone who needs it"
- Include 3–5 relevant hashtags at the end
- Close with a soft profile CTA

**Tone:** Relatable, slightly more casual than LinkedIn. Educational but with personality. Think "knowledgeable friend sharing a tip on their story."

## Short-Form Video Script (TikTok / Reels / Shorts)

**Format:** ~300 words, spoken script for 60–90 second video

**Structure guidelines:**
- Open with a scroll-stopping hook (first 3 seconds are everything): a bold claim, surprising fact, or "stop if you..." statement
- Get to the value immediately — no lengthy intros
- Structure as: Hook → Context (brief) → 2–3 actionable tips → CTA
- Write in spoken language — contractions, casual phrasing, how someone actually talks
- Include brief visual/scene direction notes in brackets where helpful (e.g., "[point at camera]", "[text overlay: Tip #1]")
- Keep energy high throughout — short sentences, quick transitions
- End with a clear CTA: "Follow for more [topic]" or "Comment [word] if you want the full breakdown"

**Tone:** High energy, direct, authentic. Like you're sharing a secret with a friend over coffee — but faster. No corporate speak. No filler.
