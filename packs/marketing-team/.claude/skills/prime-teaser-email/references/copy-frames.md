# Prime Copy Frames — Full Taxonomy

This file contains all 7 copy frames and their sub-frames. Each sub-frame includes a description and example to show the technique in action. When writing a teaser email, find the specific sub-frame the user requested and apply its technique.

## 1. CURIOSITY

Highlights a tiny, interesting gap between the prospect and their desired outcome — either an information gap or a solution gap.

### Information Gap

Demonstrates how a small, missing bit of information could hold the answer to understanding or solving a problem better.

**Paradoxical Questions:** Opens a question about the effect of a specific, missing piece of information.

**Known Fact → Why:** Introduces a known fact, then asks a question that exposes a missing piece of information.
- Example: "Why is it so hard for an adult to learn a new language, when young children have no problem learning multiple languages naturally?"

**New → Why:** Introduces a new fact, then asks a question that exposes a missing piece of information.
- Example: "Why are these 2500-year-old teeth perfectly preserved after millennia of lying in the dirt, while our teeth are practically rotting in our head with modern dentistry?"

**The Invisible Difference:** Tells a story about two drastically different outcomes where all variables are the same except one.
- Example: "These two dogs are the same age, eat the same food, exercise the same, have the same genetics — yet one has grey fur and can barely hobble up the stairs, while the other plays like a puppy. What makes the difference?"

### Solution Gap

Demonstrates how a single action, strategy, or object could solve the prospect's problem.

**System/Strategy:** Introduces a comprehensive system or strategy that can solve a problem.
- Example: "Use the Wild Card Copywriter system to become a highly paid copywriter."

**Taboo Solution:** Hints that a previously "taboo" or unexpected solution could actually solve a problem.
- Example: "Eat this 'White Carb' before bed to burn fat."

**Characterization:** Names or characterizes a hidden problem, making it tangible and solvable.
- Example: "Could solving 'Hidden Constipation' clear up your gut problems?"

**Tiny Action:** Introduces a small, simple action that promises outsized results.
- Example: "This 1-Min Stretch Reactivates Your Metabolism."

**Odd Object:** Introduces an unusual or unexpected object as the key to solving the problem.
- Example: "This 83-Page Book Allows You to Create a 7-Figure Business In Under 90 Days."

**Blind Curiosity:** Hints that some bizarre, unknown concept could hold the solution.
- Example: "How 'Quantum Linguistics' can help you master any skill in half the time."

## 2. EMOTION

Aims to evoke a singular, powerful emotion in the prospect to capture attention.

**Urgency:** Highlights a rapidly approaching event, deadline, or limited opportunity.
- Example: "Buy this crypto stock by September 3rd, 2024 to secure a 120x return."

**Scarcity:** Emphasizes that an opportunity might be running out.
- Example: "Only 100 spots left for this exclusive webinar."

**Fear:** Generates fear through an impending crisis and warns about potential danger.
- Example: "WARNING: These 6 common shampoos are linked to cancer."

**Nostalgia/Hope:** Creates a "Timeline Snapshot" highlighting the emotion of a past or future positive state.
- Example: "Think back to when you first got your puppy. Were you excited? Nervous? Think about the joy you had when you first saw them plodding across the living room. How did you feel?"

**Pride:** Emphasizes being part of a select, elite group.
- Example: "Join the top 1% of entrepreneurs who have cracked the code to success."

**Guilt:** Highlights the feeling of not doing enough for someone or something important.
- Example: "Your aging dog is depending on you."

**Anger:** Taps into the audience's sense of being wronged, mistreated, or fed up.
- Example: "Big Tech just stole your data."

## 3. SENSE-MAKING

### Secret Knowledge

Hints that some secret, buried, or lost knowledge could hold the key to solving the prospect's problem.

**Insider Confession:** An insider or whistleblower reveals a secret previously only known to an elite group.
- Example: "Ex-Banker reveals weird 'credit loophole.'"

**Secret Society:** Hints at a secret group that contains information relevant to the prospect's problems.
- Example: "Yale Secret Society's 10-Sec Manifestation Trick."

**Forgotten Wisdom:** Reveals a lost or ancient secret being rediscovered.
- Example: "Ancient Egyptian 'Candy' Flushes Out Stuck Poop."

### Analogies

Draws an analogy between the prospect's problem or solution and something interesting and easy to understand.

**Surprise Connection:** Draws an analogy between a known object and the mechanism behind the problem or solution.
- Example: "Take a look at this 'goldfish.' In a minute, I'm going to tell you how this 'goldfish' can help you double your close rate."

### Metaphor

Creates a concrete metaphor to illustrate a point about the problem or solution.

**Natural Metaphor:** Draws from something in nature.
- Example: "This is the Verzasca River. And if you know anything about joints, this is how they're SUPPOSED to look."

**Mechanical Metaphor:** Uses a mechanical or everyday object metaphor.
- Example: "Weird 'Pressure Valve' fixes high BP."

**Object Metaphor:** Takes a random object as metaphor for how a problem or solution works.
- Example: "The 'Hamburger Sales Letter' is the simplest, most effective framework for writing million dollar VSLs in just 3 steps."

### Event/Scenario

Compares an event or scenario to the dynamics of the prospect's problem or solution.

**Historical Event (Known):** Leverages a well-known past event.
- Example: "The 'Civil War' in Your Joints."

**Historical Event (Unknown):** Introduces a past event to draw an analogy.
- Example: "How Taft's bathroom can help you write better upsells."

**Current Event (Known):** Leverages a well-known current event.
- Example: "What [this week's big headline] teaches us about follow-up."

**Current Event (Unknown):** Introduces a current event to draw an analogy.
- Example: "My newborn's genius 'sales pitch.'"

**Future Event (Known):** Leverages a well-known future event.
- Example: "The 'Singularity' of Direct Response."

**Future Event (Unknown):** Introduces a future event to draw an analogy.
- Example: "What My Future Wedding Taught Me About Crypto."

### Storytelling

**Story:** Tells a story about some aspect of the sales process.
- Example: "The 200-Year-Old 'Court Ruling' That Changed Marketing Forever."

**PIG (Punch-In-the-Gut):** A shocking opening line (usually dialogue) that draws people right into the story.
- Example: "Look, the forklift just drove through the showroom window!"

**Q&A:** Provides answers from a select group of experts.
- Example: "Ask the Expert: Your Top 10 Keto Questions Answered."

**Listicle:** A curated list of information about a problem or solution.
- Example: "The 5 Best Superfoods for Thyroid."

**Comparisons:** Compares different solutions in the marketplace.
- Example: "Email vs. Text: Which One Gets Your Leads to Reply?"

## 4. PATTERN INTERRUPT

Leverages contradictory, contrarian, and bold statements to create attention by standing out from other opinions. The shocking, controversial, and borderline taboo statements make the prospect pay attention.

### Contrarian Claim

**Small Belief:** Contradicts a "minor" belief the market might hold.
- Example: "Constipated? Stop eating broccoli."

**Big Belief:** Contradicts a "major," crystallized belief about a problem or how to solve it.
- Example: "Why AI Will Never Write Good Copy."

**System/Philosophy:** Contradicts an entire worldview or philosophy in the marketplace.
- Example: "The Disastrous Failure of Keto."

**Person/Group:** Contradicts the viewpoint of a specific person or group.
- Example: "Why the Hustle Gurus Are Wrong About Your Mornings."

### Other Pattern Interrupts

**Soundbite Stunner:** A quote or statement that triggers an emotional response related to the prospect's most pressing problems. Typically pivots into a story so it's not stated directly at the prospect.
- Example: "You're the Bottleneck."

**Bold Proclamation:** A bold statement that's controversial because of how absolute it is.
- Example: "Why You Will Never Scale on Referrals Alone."

**Controversial Opinion:** States a controversial position on a topic the market cares about.
- Example: "Why I Ban Freebie Seekers From My Email List."

**Elephant in the Room:** Addresses a sensitive or rarely discussed issue.
- Example: "The Dark Side of the Self-Help Industry."

## 5. GIFT

**Hack/Tip:** Offers to reveal an important hack, tip, or bit of information.
- Example: "My #1 Pickup Line (Works on Autopilot)."

**Gift:** Offers a tangible good (tool, resource, support, trial, sample, etc.).
- Example: "steal our 9-figure copy-checklist (free)."

---

## 6. INTERACTIVE

Directly invites the reader to engage in a thinking process.

### Quiz

**Diagnostic:** Invites the prospect to take a quiz to discover if they have a problem.
- Example: "Do you have 'toxic liver'?"

**Good/Bad Things:** Reveals a potentially "good" thing actually being bad, or vice versa.
- Example: "[Quiz] Which vegetable KILLS your t-levels?"

### Other Interactive

**Challenge:** Directly challenges the prospect's skills and understanding.
- Example: "Can You Spot the 5 Mistakes in This Email Marketing Campaign?"

**Poll:** Invites participation in a poll.
- Example: "Vote Now: What's Your Biggest Struggle with Weight Loss?"

**Look Closer:** Invites the prospect to examine an image, video, or idea more closely to spot a hidden solution.
- Example: "Look closely at this ancient Egyptian hieroglyphic. Hidden in this photo is a constipation solution so powerful it recently won a Nobel Prize."

## 7. SOCIAL / TOPICAL

Leverages the prospect's innate interest in breaking news, social experiments, topical events, and other people's opinions.

**Breaking News:** Leverages "breaking news" to gather attention.
- Example: "BREAKING NEWS: Google's AI Bombshell."

**Virality:** Leverages the fact that a product or idea went viral.
- Example: "This 'eye serum' sold out in Target in 42 seconds."

**Controversy/Gossip:** Leverages controversy or gossip about people relevant to the prospect.
- Example: "I just reported Stefan Georgi to the FTC."

**Great Experiment:** Leverages a big, "hard to fake" experiment or test.
- Example: "I tested 14,356 of the best skincare products."

**Social Proof:** Leverages the fact that many others have used a solution.
- Example: "over 8k+ women lost fat with this."

**Case Study:** Highlights a real-life example of someone who successfully used the product or solution.
- Example: "How This Single Mom Lost 50 Pounds in 90 Days."

**New Discovery:** Connects a new discovery with insight into the problem or solution.
- Example: "Harvard scientists discover new fat-switch."

**Authority:** Leverages the well-known authority of someone the prospects know.
- Example: "What a 9-figure CEO just cut from their calendar?"

**Memes & Zeitgeist:** Leverages a current event, popular meme, or cultural phenomenon.
- Example: "[This month's viral meme] client outreach."

## Combining Frames

Frames can and should be combined for maximum effect. Common combinations:

- **Curiosity + Emotion:** Tease information gap while triggering urgency or fear
- **Sense-Making + Curiosity:** Use a metaphor or analogy to frame the curiosity mechanism
- **Pattern Interrupt + Social/Topical:** Bold contrarian claim backed by social proof or case study
- **Emotion + Social/Topical:** Urgency or fear combined with breaking news or authority

When combining, one frame should lead (the structural frame for the email's hook) and the other should support (woven into the body or CTA).
