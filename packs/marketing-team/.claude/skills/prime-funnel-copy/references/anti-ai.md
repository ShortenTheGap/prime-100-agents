# Anti-AI Quality Filter

Run this scan as a hard filter before delivering ANY copy to the user. If even one item is hit, rewrite before delivery. No exceptions.

This is the single highest-leverage filter for attracting the correct buyer. Sophisticated buyers, the ones with money to spend, bounce hard from copy that smells like AI. Filtering ruthlessly here is the difference between attracting price-sensitive tire-kickers and attracting your ideal buyer.

---

## FORBIDDEN WORD LIST (delete on sight)

journey, navigate, embark, delve, dive, unleash, unlock, leverage, utilize, meticulous, elevate, harness, realm, fascinating, profound, groundbreaking, revolutionary, innovative, disruptive, insights, tapestry, craft, blueprint, transform, paradigm, unprecedented, beacon, moreover, furthermore, consequently, ultimately, essentially, simply, arguably, firstly, secondly, thirdly, in conclusion, in summary, optimize, drive results, implement, seamlessly, effortlessly, streamline, robust, comprehensive, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, empower

## FORBIDDEN PATTERNS (rewrite if found)

### 1. Triplet Patterns (three things in a row)
❌ "No more empty slots in your calendar. No more price-sensitive tire-kickers. No more margin-killing discounts."

### 2. Question + List Answers
❌ "What do they have instead? Stories that grab attention. Questions that create tension. And offers that actually make sense."

### 3. "The Result?" + List
❌ "The result? Stress that had my doctor concerned. Burnout that made me resent trading. And returns that barely beat a basic index fund."

### 4. "Even If" Series
❌ "Even if you're low on time. Even if you hate marketing. Even if you've tried other stuff before."

### 5. "This Isn't Just X, It's Y"
❌ "This isn't just motivational fluff — it's the cold, mathematical reality of trading psychology."

### 6. "It's Not X, It's Y"
❌ "It's not because they're 'better builders' than you. They just have better marketing systems."

### 7. "Something Fascinating" Setup
❌ "I was talking to my buddy Mike (total history buff) over beers last night, and he pointed out something fascinating..."
❌ "But here's where it gets interesting..."

### 8. "Look, I Don't Need to Tell You" Transition
❌ "Look, I don't need to tell you that building is getting tougher."

### 9. "Brutal Truth" Phrases
❌ "Brutal, right?" / "The brutal truth is..." / "Here's the brutal reality..."

### 10. "They Don't / They Do" Parallel Structures
❌ "They don't have fancy graphics. Sometimes they have typos. And they definitely don't use words like 'leverage'..."

### 11. Specific 2-4 AM Time References
❌ "Staring at the ceiling at 2:47 AM" / "Woke up at 3:00 AM thinking about..."
(Any oddly specific time between 2-4 AM is an instant AI tell.)

### 12. Suspiciously Structured Phrases
❌ "Not just X, but Y"
❌ "Whether X or Y"
❌ "This means that..."
❌ "Let's explore" / "Let's dive into"
❌ "Picture this:" / "Imagine this:"
❌ "The key is:" / "The truth is:"

### 13. Excessive Sentence Fragments Without Pronouns
❌ "Got a text yesterday from a builder."
❌ "Threw in a video where I walk."
❌ "Sent an email. Got a response. Made a call."
(Strategic fragments are fine. Staccato strings of them are an AI tell.)

### 14. Overly Symmetrical Structures
❌ "When you X, you Y. When you Z, you A."
❌ "If you want X, you need Y. If you want Z, you need A."

### 15. Suspiciously Perfect Transitions
❌ "Now that we've covered X, let's move on to Y."
❌ "With that in mind, let's consider..."
❌ "Having established X, we can now examine Y."

### 16. Formatting Errors
❌ Em dashes, en dashes, or hyphens used as dashes. Use a comma, a period, or a colon instead.
❌ Overusing colons in headlines
❌ Non-standard bullet points
❌ Mechanical line breaks

### 17. AI Paragraph Patterns
❌ Statement followed by colon introducing a list: "Email marketing is essential for business growth: it builds relationships, increases conversions, and provides results."
❌ Perfectly balanced both-sides arguments: "While some argue X, others point out Y..."
❌ Symmetric paragraph structure: "First, we need to consider X. This is important because... Second, we need to address Y. This matters because..."

## SUPERIOR HUMAN WRITING CHARACTERISTICS

After scrubbing AI tells, layer these in:

- Musical flow with varied sentence structures: long, medium, short
- Run-on sentences when excited, not caring about grammar
- Sometimes starting mid-thought without context
- Mixes punchy declarations with longer wandering sentences
- Direct, unhedged claims: no "perhaps" / "it could be argued"
- Specific numbers, names, times, places: never round numbers like "10 ways"; use "13 ways" or "7 ways"
- Goes off on tangents then returns to the point
- Sometimes forgets to finish a sentence
- Uses "and" where "but" would be grammatically correct (maintains momentum)
- References personal experiences that can't be googled
- Cultural references that aren't obvious or universal
- Strategic occasional fragments (not as a default pattern)
- Shows emotion: irritation, excitement, confusion
- Occasional cursing or slang when the voice supports it
- Vulnerability: admits mistakes or limitations
- Analogies that aren't clichéd

## PATTERN INTERRUPTS THAT WORK (use these intentionally)

### Mid-Sentence Reset
✅ "Email marketing gives you a ton of benefits for... you know what, let me back up. Here's why email actually matters."

### Sudden Question
✅ "Sending emails on a regular schedule helps keep your subscribers engaged and... wait, are you actually sending emails regularly? Because if not, the rest of this won't matter much."

### Casual Admission
✅ "These strategies have worked really well across different businesses. (Though I completely botched the implementation the first time I tried it myself.)"

### Deliberate Tangent
✅ "Your subject line makes or breaks your open rate. I learned this the hard way last year when my dog chewed through my laptop charger the night before a big launch and I had to write all my emails on my phone. The rushed subject lines TANKED."

### Thought Correction
✅ "The problem with most email strategies is they focus too much on... no, scratch that. The real issue is they don't focus enough on the reader's problems."

## HUMAN FORMATTING PATTERNS

- Mix of short and long paragraphs
- Occasional one-sentence paragraphs for emphasis
- Random use of formatting for emphasis
- Strategic sentence fragments (not overused)
- Starting sentences with And, But, So
- Contractions (don't, can't, won't, I'm)
- Casual interjections
- Parentheticals that add personality (not just information)
- Self-deprecating comments
- References to personal quirks or habits
- Ellipses and other punctuation used inconsistently (never em or en dashes)
- Occasional ALL CAPS for emphasis (not overused)
- No em dashes or en dashes: commas, periods, and colons instead

## EXAMPLES OF WRITING THAT PASSES

> "Copy chiefing is broken. (In my humble opinion, at least). And that's because of how MOST PEOPLE DO IT."

> "Forget open rates for a second. What we're REALLY after is getting people to actually care about your emails. (Weird concept, I know.)"

> "I'd wake up in the middle of the night to see what futures were doing in Asia. As if my checking would change anything."

## FINAL VERIFICATION CHECKLIST

Before delivering copy, verify ALL of these:

- [ ] Sounds like a specific person talking, not a generic "professional voice"
- [ ] Contains opinions some people might disagree with
- [ ] Has rhythm variations, not the same beat throughout
- [ ] Zero words from the forbidden list
- [ ] None of the 17 forbidden patterns
- [ ] Zero em dashes or en dashes (commas, periods, colons instead)
- [ ] Specific details that aren't googlable
- [ ] At least one strategic incomplete sentence or fragment (without overusing)
- [ ] At least one pattern interrupt
- [ ] Reads naturally aloud
- [ ] Conversational flow, not perfect structure
- [ ] Zero instances of "Indeed," "Moreover," "Furthermore"
- [ ] No business jargon or buzzwords

If any item fails, rewrite that section. Then re-scan.
