# Charlie Munger: Skills Roadmap (Phase Two)

> Index of the discrete skills that give Charlie his hands. Each becomes `skills/<skill-name>/SKILL.md`, loaded on top of the core skill. Status: **proposed** until built. Agree the list, then build in the order below.

Each entry: what it produces, when it triggers, the Munger doctrine it rests on (dossier section), and the modern layer we add.

---

## Tier 1: the foundation (build first; everything else depends on them)

### 1. `cash-runway-review`
- **Produces:** a cash position, monthly burn or free cash, runway in months, a 13-week cash view, the minimum-reserve check against the member's standing rule, and the three moves that most extend runway.
- **Triggers:** "cash," "cash flow," "runway," "how long can I last," "am I okay," "tight month," "can I make payroll"; also auto-runs when any spend decision arrives with no current cash figure.
- **Doctrine:** §4.9 (avoid ruin; liquidity first), §4.10 (cash is the truth), Wesco and Berkshire practice of holding cash to act when others cannot.
- **Modern layer:** 13-week cash flow format, receivables ageing, deferred revenue for prepaid programs, card and payment-processor reserves, data pulled from Xero/QuickBooks through Manny's connection.

### 2. `unit-economics`
- **Produces:** per-offer price, cost to deliver, gross margin, contribution margin, CAC by channel, payback months, LTV, and a one-line judgment on which offers make money and which only make revenue.
- **Triggers:** "margin," "unit economics," "CAC," "LTV," "payback," "which offer makes money," "is this profitable," "cost per client."
- **Doctrine:** §4.6 (quality of the economics), §4.7 (opportunity cost), §4.2 (arithmetic and accounting as base models).
- **Modern layer:** CAC/LTV and payback vocabulary (post-Munger), owner's labor priced at market rate, cohort retention, ad-platform spend attribution caveats.

### 3. `decision-memo`
- **Produces:** a one to two page memo: the decision, fact sheet, alternatives, inversion, models applied, verdict (yes / no / too hard), conditions, cheapest test, check-back date.
- **Triggers:** "should I," "help me decide," "is this worth it," "pros and cons," any decision with a meaningful cost or commitment.
- **Doctrine:** §4.11 (Practical Thought five notions), §4.12 (two-track analysis and checklists), §4.1.
- **Modern layer:** decision journal format so outcomes can be compared with the verdict later; stored in memory with check-back dates.

### 4. `inversion-premortem`
- **Produces:** a pre-mortem: "it is a year from now and this failed; why," ranked failure paths with likelihood and damage, fatal-path flag, leading indicators, and mitigations.
- **Triggers:** "what could go wrong," "pre-mortem," "poke holes," "devil's advocate," "am I missing something," before any launch, hire, deal, or loan.
- **Doctrine:** §4.1 in full (Jacobi; the 1986 misery prescriptions; Practical Thought notion five).
- **Modern layer:** Gary Klein's pre-mortem technique (credited as such), risk-scoring matrix, early-warning metrics.

### 5. `pl-review`
- **Produces:** a review of P&L, balance sheet, and cash flow statement: what the business really earns after paying the owner a market wage, margin trends, cost creep, concentration, accounting red flags, and three ranked changes.
- **Triggers:** member pastes or uploads financial statements; "review my P&L," "look at my numbers," "what do you see," "month-end," "year-end."
- **Doctrine:** §4.10 (accounting skepticism), §4.6, CLAUDE.md review mode.
- **Modern layer:** cash vs accrual distinction for small businesses, add-back scrutiny, owner's discretionary earnings, bookkeeping hygiene checks; tax items routed to the CPA.

## Tier 2: the money decisions

### 6. `pricing-economics`
- **Produces:** price floor from cost to deliver and target margin, value-to-customer estimate, pricing-power test design (raise for new clients first, watch close rate and churn), and the economics of discounts and payment plans.
- **Triggers:** "what should I charge," "raise prices," "am I underpriced," "discount," "payment plan," "pricing power."
- **Doctrine:** §4.6 (See's Candies; pricing power as the test of a business), §5 (See's case), USC 1994.
- **Modern layer:** tiered offers, value metrics, price-test sequencing; hands presentation and copy to David Ogilvy.

### 7. `hire-economics`
- **Produces:** fully loaded cost of the role, runway impact, what the role must return and by when, break-even, contractor or part-time alternative, and the incentive structure for the role.
- **Triggers:** "should I hire," "can I afford a," "first employee," "VA," "salesperson," "ops manager," "contractor vs employee."
- **Doctrine:** §4.7 (opportunity cost), §4.9 (fixed costs and ruin), §4.5 (incentives).
- **Modern layer:** payroll tax and benefits loading (with "confirm with your CPA"), ramp time, role design handed to Peter Drucker; employment classification flagged for the attorney.

### 8. `deal-evaluation`
- **Produces:** evaluation of an acquisition, partnership, joint venture, licence, large contract, or outside investment: Berkshire-style filters, valuation sanity check, what could go wrong, incentive alignment of the counterparty, and terms to insist on or walk from.
- **Triggers:** "deal," "buy this business," "partner with," "JV," "someone wants to invest," "equity," "big contract," "licence."
- **Doctrine:** §4.7 (the four filters), §4.6 (wonderful business at a fair price), §4.4 (circle of competence), §5 (See's, Blue Chip, Wesco).
- **Modern layer:** small-business valuation multiples as rough guides only, earnout and seller-note risk; all legal, securities, and tax structure explicitly referred to attorney and CPA.

### 9. `incentive-plan-review`
- **Produces:** review or design of commissions, bonuses, profit shares, partner splits, and contractor terms: what behavior each rewards, the gaming risks, and a redesign tied to the result the member actually wants (usually margin and retention, not raw revenue).
- **Triggers:** "commission," "comp plan," "bonus," "profit share," "partner split," "how should I pay my," "my team isn't motivated."
- **Doctrine:** §4.5 in full (Federal Express, Xerox, cost-plus), §4.3 (reward and punishment superresponse; incentive-caused bias).
- **Modern layer:** clawbacks, gross-margin-based commissions, vesting; employment-law and tax points flagged for professionals.

### 10. `risk-register`
- **Produces:** "what would kill this business": a ranked register of existential and serious risks (cash, concentration, key person, platform dependency, debt, legal, reputation), likelihood, severity, mitigation, owner, review date.
- **Triggers:** "what would kill this business," "biggest risks," "what keeps you up," "am I exposed," annual planning, after any near-miss.
- **Doctrine:** §4.9 (avoid ruin), §4.1 (inversion), §4.2 (engineering backup systems, margin of safety).
- **Modern layer:** platform and algorithm dependency, payment-processor holds, cyber and data risk, insurance review prompts (referred to a broker).

## Tier 3: planning, judgment, and recurring work

### 11. `forecast-builder`
- **Produces:** a 12-month revenue, cost, and cash forecast with base, bad, and very bad cases, every assumption labelled and sourced, and the month the very bad case breaches the minimum reserve.
- **Triggers:** "forecast," "budget," "plan for next year," "projections," "what if revenue drops," "model this."
- **Doctrine:** §4.10 (distrust optimistic numbers), §4.3 (overoptimism tendency), §4.1.
- **Modern layer:** spreadsheet output via the xlsx skill if present, scenario toggles, lender-ready versions that stay accurate.

### 12. `capital-allocation`
- **Produces:** a ranking of uses for spare cash (reserve, debt paydown, reinvestment in proven channels, new initiatives, owner distributions) by return, risk, and reversibility, against the member's best existing alternative.
- **Triggers:** "what should I do with the profit," "pay down debt or invest," "where should the money go," "owner draw," "good month, now what."
- **Doctrine:** §4.7 (opportunity cost as yardstick), §4.8 (few big decisions, patience), Berkshire capital allocation practice.
- **Modern layer:** owner distributions vs reinvestment trade-off for small businesses; personal investing explicitly out of scope and referred to a licensed adviser.

### 13. `misjudgment-check`
- **Produces:** a bias audit of a decision the member is leaning toward: which of the 25 tendencies are active, evidence for each, the lollapalooza warning when several combine, and the question to ask that would break the spell.
- **Triggers:** "I really want to do this," "everyone is doing it," "I've already put so much into," "I can't miss this," "my gut says," or when Charlie detects strong emotion around a money decision.
- **Doctrine:** §4.3 in full (the 25 tendencies), §4.12 (two-track analysis).
- **Modern layer:** decision-hygiene prompts (cooling-off period, outside view, base rates); kept respectful, never a diagnosis of the person.

### 14. `offer-economics`
- **Produces:** the economics under an offer or campaign David Ogilvy is building: break-even sales, allowable CAC, ad-spend ceiling, refund and guarantee cost, delivery capacity at volume, and a go / revise / stop line.
- **Triggers:** "will this launch make money," "how much can I spend on ads," "break-even," "guarantee cost," a David campaign brief needing numbers.
- **Doctrine:** §4.6, §4.7, §4.9; team boundary (Charlie judges, David writes).
- **Modern layer:** ROAS and blended CAC, refund-rate assumptions, payment-plan default risk; handoff format David reads.

### 15. `monthly-cfo-review`
- **Produces:** a recurring one-page month-end pack: cash and runway, margin by offer, concentration, variance to forecast, decisions due for check-back, and the one thing to do this month.
- **Triggers:** "month-end," "monthly review," "how did we do," "CFO update," a scheduled recurring run.
- **Doctrine:** §4.10, §4.12 (checklists), §4.8 (patience; avoid needless activity).
- **Modern layer:** pulls from accounting connections Manny sets up; can be scheduled as a loop; stays a draft for the member.

---

## Build order and rationale

1 to 5 first: every other skill needs the cash position, the unit economics, the memo format, the pre-mortem, and the ability to read statements. 6 to 10 next: these are the decisions members most often bring and where the most money is lost. 11 to 15 last: they turn one-off judgment into planning and a recurring habit.

## Conventions for every Charlie skill

- Frontmatter `name` and a pushy `description` that lists the trigger phrases.
- Body under 500 lines; deeper material in `references/`.
- Every method step tagged with its dossier section so it can be checked and challenged.
- A hand-back template ending in **What I am unsure of / What would change my mind / Cheapest test first / Numbers I need / For your CPA or attorney**.
- Explicit "modern layer" notes marked as translation from Munger's Berkshire-scale practice.
- Never moves money, never fabricates a number, never gives tax, legal, or securities advice.
- No em dashes or en dashes.
